# Festival sources and confidence — audit 001

Reviewed: 2026-11-07.

## Result

- 39 festival nodes; 126 festival-level references after the Wikipedia pass (86 after the first review, compared with the original 52 references for 37 nodes).
- Every festival has at least three distinct source URLs.
- 33 High and six Medium festival entries; 120 High and three Medium festival associations.
- Added Pythian Games, Three Choirs, four places and five sourced associations.

## What the ratings mean

High means the retained core facts are directly supported and corroborated by credible references. Medium records material conventional dating, unresolved discrepancies, limited references or contextual interpretation. These are editorial grades, not statistical probabilities. Confidence was reassessed for every festival and association; adding a link does not automatically improve the grade.

## Review method and limits

The pass used retrieved web text, archival/catalogue records and indexed excerpts/abstracts. Full books, paywalled chapters and every linked PDF were not read in full. Search excerpts can verify a limited date or identifying fact without supporting the rest of a book. Programme listings establish announced participation, which is different from independently witnessing a performance.

URLs, publishers and independent evidence are different measures. An operator and its owner are related; a university hosting an old journal is not endorsing every statement; tourism bodies can be promotional; archives can corroborate an appearance without proving an origin date. The ledger records the additional source role and its limited scope.

## Material findings

- Cape Town: contemporary reporting places the CTICC move in 2004, while the operator’s retrospective says 2005. The graph retains the supported 2000 start and does not assert the disputed relocation year.
- Reading: the museum documents the 1961 Richmond precursor and 1971 arrival in Reading. Removed the broad Guinness first-pop-festival framing in favour of the museum account.
- Sawai Gandharva: removed a promotional site with inconsistent chronology. The retained collectors’ journal and government-commissioned draft support 1953/Pune; the draft contains other biographical errors and cannot establish all retrospective claims.
- Festival au Désert: replaced the unofficial dedicated site with university and scholarly-society accounts; exact early sites and the evolving exile/touring scope remain qualified.
- Sónar: removed the production-admin endpoint and redundant owner page; an academic case study complements the public festival archive.
- Coachella: replaced an unretrievable documentary endpoint with the Recording Academy account and narrowed the entry to supported inaugural facts.
- Pythian Games: musical contests predate the conventional 586–582 BCE reform/numbering chronology. The revised approximate 424 CE upper display anchor follows scholarly discussion of probable ongoing games, rather than the museum’s traditional 393/394 account. It is not a proven final edition. This is not evidence of the first music festival anywhere.
- Three Choirs: about 1715 is a retrospective counting convention, not a proven first meeting; the current name dates to 1838. Long-surviving classical tradition is not equivalent to earliest recorded festival worldwide.
- Fuji Rock 1997: artist links describe programme listings; cancellation means a listing alone does not prove every scheduled act performed.
