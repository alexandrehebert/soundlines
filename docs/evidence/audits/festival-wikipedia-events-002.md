# Festival Wikipedia checks and History events — review 002

Reviewed: 2026-11-07.

## Result

All 39 festival articles were retrieved and compared with the existing graph. The Spanish Petronio Álvarez and French FESPAM articles were used where appropriate. Twenty-one source-backed History events were added; the three existing festival events were reused. The graph now has 103 History events and 123 festival associations. Festival-level references total 126, with at least three URLs per festival.

## Source policy

Wikipedia is supplementary and can reuse the same references as the graph. Article maintenance warnings, an infobox, and a larger URL count do not establish independent evidence or a confidence increase. Available revision permalinks are recorded in the JSON; otherwise the URL and review date identify the retrieved page. No missing revision ID was invented.

Event selection is editorial: require a dated performance, premiere, institutional format or circulation mechanism with a source-backed historical reason. Not selecting a separate event is not a claim of historical unimportance. Dates, entry confidence and landmark/notable significance remain separate judgments.

## Discrepancies resolved or retained

- Pythian Games: Wikipedia’s 424 CE reference prompted follow-up research. A 2021 scholarly discussion of late-Roman cities cites evidence of games at Delphi in 424 and says the Pythian games were probably still ongoing. The estimated display endpoint is now 424, replacing the traditional 393/394 account. Neither value is asserted as a proven last edition.
- Petronio Álvarez: the checked Spanish article gives 1996, while the broadcasting archive and university history support 1997. Retain the corroborated first-edition date and expose the conflict.
- Baalbeck: distinguish 1955 precursor activity from the named institution and committee in 1956.
- WOMAD, Tomorrowland and Wacken: distinguish organisation/planning/idea dates from the first festival edition.
- Movement: distinguish the 2000 DEMF from later names, producers and the current Movement organisation.
- Cape Town: retain the already documented 2004/2005 venue disagreement; Wikipedia aligns with the contemporary 2004 account.
- Sónar: an obituary-style 2026 announcement is interpreted as closure in Wikipedia, while the checked operator archive advertises 2027. Do not assert permanent closure from that unresolved claim.
- Regional traditions: do not map Chilean Nueva Ola onto the catalogue’s unrelated New Wave genre, or ancient translated instrument names onto a modern electric guitar.
