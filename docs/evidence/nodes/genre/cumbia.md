## Sources checked

- [Colombian Ministry of Culture, Traditional Cumbia of the Colombian Caribbean](https://www.mincultura.gov.co/direcciones/patrimonio-y-memoria/Paginas/servicios-informacion/LRPCI/cumbia-tradicional-del-caribe-colombiano.aspx) — Official heritage account defines cumbia as music, dance, and social practice rooted in the Caribbean region and Bajo Magdalena, formed through Indigenous, African, and European cultural processes; it does not assign an origin year.
- [Journal of the American Musicological Society, review of The Invention of Latin American Music](https://online.ucpress.edu/jams/article/74/3/659/119246/The-Invention-of-Latin-American-Music-A) — Musicological discussion says cumbia crystallized as a market category in Colombia in the 1940s and warns against treating contested origin narratives as settled genre history.
- [Smithsonian Folkways, Colombian music liner notes](https://folkways-media.si.edu/docs/folkways/artwork/SFW40531.pdf) — Annotated recording history describes coastal community origins, the 1940s urban dance-hall uptake, and commercial spread beyond Colombia while local traditions continued.
- [SAGE, “Yo Me Llamo Cumbia”](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/26349795241291347) — Recent academic paper cites scholarship placing cumbia’s formation as early as the late 18th century and highlights Afro-Indigenous women and oral tradition; this is a scholarly position, not a universally fixed date.

## Assessment

The old 1900 start and 1915 “golden-age” marker had no support in the cited AllMusic page. Shifted the broad origin marker to circa 1800 with explicit uncertainty, reflecting scholarship placing formation in the late 18th century, and distinguished that older, locally varied practice from the 1940s commercial genre category. Smithsonian Folkways independently corroborates the 1940s orchestral uptake and transnational spread while emphasizing continuity of community traditions. Removed all phases: Cumbia is a diverse transnational family with no defensible single prominence curve; the old 2010 “established” phase was not evidence-based.

## Remaining uncertainty

A precise origin year cannot be established from these sources: oral tradition, later written documentation, and twentieth-century genre marketing describe different historical layers. The circa-1800 graph start is a low-precision approximation, not a claim of a single invention date. Colombian traditional cumbia and later national/urban variants should not be conflated.
