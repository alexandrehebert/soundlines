## Sources checked

- [Marcia Peres, A Respectable Spell: Transformations of Samba in Rio de Janeiro](https://academic.oup.com/illinois-scholarship-online/book/42538/chapter-abstract/356853943) — Academic history treats 1917 and Pelo telefone as the moment samba became widely successful as a named recorded genre, rather than its complete origin.
- [Barbara Browning, Samba: Resistance in Motion / Making Samba](https://www.jstor.org/stable/j.ctv113155n) — Scholarly history details Afro-Brazilian practices, the 1916 registration and the contested cultural construction of the samba category.
- [Musicabrasilis, Pelo Telefone and the history of samba](https://musicabrasilis.org.br/pt-br/artigos/pelo-telefone-e-historia-do-samba/) — Research-based specialist essay reviews contemporary press, National Library copyright and recording archives; notes disputed authorship and genre labels.
- [Cambridge, Bossa Nova/Novo Brasil research article](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/FC062BDD4B2388F51877525A5914EE8D/S0023879100033665a.pdf/bossa_novanovo_brasil_the_significance_of_bossa_nova_as_a_brazilian_popular_music.pdf) — Academic history cross-checks the 1917 recording as a widely circulated early samba milestone.

## Assessment

Changed the loose 1910s start to c.1917 as a documented public/commercial consolidation milestone, not the birth of all samba practices. Historical scholarship foregrounds Afro-Brazilian community practices and debates whether Pelo telefone is the “first samba”; its role in popularizing and naming recorded samba is well supported. Removed arbitrary phases; samba is a broad, living family with regional forms. Confidence: medium-high for the 1917 milestone, low for treating it as an absolute origin.

## Remaining uncertainty

Describe 1917 as a landmark in recorded Carioca samba; earlier practices and claims of first composition/recording remain contested.
