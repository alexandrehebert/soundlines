## Sources checked

- [Library of Congress, Jazz Singers exhibition](https://www.loc.gov/exhibits/jazz-singers/overview.html) — National collection exhibition documents vocal jazz from the 1920s to present and varied interpretive practices.
- [Smithsonian Folkways, Jazz Vol. 4: Jazz Singers](https://folkways.si.edu/jazz-vol-4-jazz-singers/ragtime/music/album/smithsonian) — Catalog highlights active vocalists of the 1920s, 1930s and 1940s, cross-checking early span.
- [Smithsonian, Billie Holiday](https://www.si.edu/spotlight/billie-holiday?page=1) — Museum bio documents Holiday’s 1930s breakthrough and interpretive influence.
- [Smithsonian, Ella Fitzgerald](https://music.si.edu/feature/ella-fitzgerald) — Institutional biography provides independent vocalist and scat/improvisation context.

## Assessment

Moved start from 1930 decade to c.1920 because the Library of Congress covers jazz singing from its 1920s beginnings. Removed a false 1945 end and 2010 established marker. Kept a broad 1930s prominence marker in the phase note, supported by the documented rise of Holiday and Fitzgerald; it is not a statistical peak measurement. Confidence: medium-high for the early period, lower for defining vocal jazz as a separate genre versus jazz performance practice.

## Remaining uncertainty

Vocal jazz spans many styles and eras; no single origin event or popularity lifecycle is appropriate.
