## Sources checked

- [Jamaica Information Service — Jamaica’s Heritage in Music](https://jis.gov.jm/information/jamaicas-heritage-dance-music/jamaicas-heritage-music/) — Government heritage overview dates deejaying’s rise to the 1970s and flowering in the 1980s; distinguishes the venue from the music.
- [Stolzoff, Wake the Town and Tell the People (Duke UP)](https://www.dukeupress.edu/wake-the-town-and-tell-the-people) — Ethnomusicological history situates dancehall culture in a longer sound-system and post-independence history.
- [Banton, Between Two Giant Sounds (University of New Orleans thesis)](https://scholarworks.uno.edu/td/508/) — Academic thesis examines reggae-to-dancehall transition during 1974–84, supporting a decade-level emergence rather than precise 1979 onset.
- [Manuel & Marshall, The Riddim Method](https://www.wayneandwax.com/academic/manuel-marshall_riddim-method.pdf) — Peer-reviewed ethnomusicology article identifies 1985 Sleng Teng as a landmark in the shift to digital riddim production.
- [Stanley Niaah, DanceHall (University of Ottawa Press)](https://press.uottawa.ca/en/9780776619057/dancehall/) — Scholarly monograph describes later-20th-century popularization and transnational performance networks.

## Assessment

Sources support dancehall’s development through the 1970s and 1980s, but not the graph’s previous precise 1979 start. Removed that precision and the unsupported 1993 golden-age and 2010 established markers. Kept an editorial stage for the documented 1985 digital watershed, and a lower-confidence stage for early-1990s international diffusion.

## Remaining uncertainty

Dancehall may mean venue, performance culture, or recording genre; those chronologies differ. International spread is gradual, so the 1990 marker is approximate. The “golden-age” phase label should be treated as stylistic editorial shorthand, not a popularity statistic.
