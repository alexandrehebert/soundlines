## Sources checked

- [Turner, “A History of Deep House” (Toolroom Academy)](https://toolroomacademy.com/features/a-history-of-deep-house/) — Electronic-music industry history dates Chicago emergence to the 1980s, identifies Larry Heard’s 1985 “Mystery of Love,” and traces early-to-mid-1990s development and later producers. Useful scene account, not neutral academic history.
- [Do You Remember House? Chicago’s Queer of Color Undergrounds (Oxford University Press)](https://academic.oup.com/book/5894/chapter-abstract/149191641) — Academic chapter abstract traces how Chicago radio, labels and DJ stores helped codify house and spread it beyond Chicago in the second half of the 1980s; full chapter fetch blocked.
- [This Is Our House: House Music, Cultural Spaces and Technologies](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9780429643576_A35837177/preview-9780429643576_A35837177.pdf) — Ethnographic study notes house’s Black and Latino cultural context in Chicago/New York and states that 1990s Chicago deep house was still produced, largely for audiences elsewhere.
- [Red Bull Music Academy — Larry Heard on Making His First House Tracks](https://web03125.microsites03.redbull.com/2017/02/larry-heard-fingers-inc/) — Interview with Heard dates “Mystery of Love” to 1984 and describes its creation and circulation through Chicago DJs; cautions against treating a single origin story as settled.

## Assessment

Replaced Wikipedia, a station genre page, and an artist page with an academic ethnography, a scholarly OUP chapter record, a producer interview, and a specific genre history. Broadened start from precise 1985 to decade-level 1980s; RBMA dates the influential recording to 1984 while Toolroom identifies its 1985 release. Removed 1989 golden-age and 1993 niche micro-boundaries; retained only broad 1990s defining period with medium confidence.

## Remaining uncertainty

“Mystery of Love” recording and commercial release dates differ in these sources (1984 vs 1985). Deep house codified gradually, and artist-centered origin narratives understate the collective Chicago scene. OUP full text unavailable in this check.
