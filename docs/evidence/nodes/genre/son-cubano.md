## Sources checked

- [PBS, Musical Atlas of Cuba: Son](https://www.pbs.org/buenavista/music/a_son.html) — Cuban music reference discusses disputed origins and early eastern Cuban examples, cautioning against a single uncontested founding date.
- [Smithsonian Folkways, Sextetos Cubanos: Sones 1930](https://folkways.si.edu/sextetos-cubanos/world/music/album/smithsonian) — Institutional catalog describes son cubano as originating in eastern Cuba in the late 19th century.
- [USC Scalar, Cuban Music in America](https://scalar.usc.edu/works/music-in-global-america/cuban-music-in-america.5) — Historical teaching resource describes son as a synthesis of Afro-Cuban and Spanish-derived traditions that urbanized by the 1920s.
- [Smithsonian National Museum of American History, Latin Jazz Timeline](https://www.americanhistory.si.edu/explore/projects/smithsonian-jazz/collections-and-archives/latin-jazz/timeline) — Institutional timeline contextualizes son as a synthesis and as national Cuban music.

## Assessment

Retained c.1890 as a broad late-19th-century eastern Cuba origin marker, not a securely documented first performance. Existing 1950 “established” phase is an unsupported popularity label and has been removed; modern son remains active. Confidence: medium because experts dispute early repertory and the often-repeated Ma Teodora origin story.

## Remaining uncertainty

Further source note should preserve the distinction between eastern Cuban early son, later urban son and the modern recorded style.
