## Sources checked

- [The Producer’s Survival Guide — Routledge / Bobby Owsinski](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9781315519760_A35076121/preview-9781315519760_A35076121.pdf) — Book introduction defines EDM as a catch-all that caught on in the late 2000s, particularly around commercial festival music; distinguishes this usage from older dance styles.
- [“How Rave Music Conquered America” — The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/music/2012/aug/02/edm-dance-music) — Journalist Simon Reynolds contextualizes U.S. industry adoption and rebranding of rave culture; supplementary to the published book’s definition.

## Assessment

The entry’s existing description already limited EDM to the modern commercial label, but one AllMusic overview was insufficient to justify the phase boundaries. A production-history reference and Reynolds’ contemporaneous account support late-2000s U.S. industry use; phases were removed because they imply a measurable arc the sources do not establish.

## Remaining uncertainty

EDM remains a contested, market-dependent umbrella. Use this chronology only for the contemporary North American commercial usage, not electronic dance music as a general practice.
