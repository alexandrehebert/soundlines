## Sources checked
- [AllMusic: Honky-tonk style](https://www.allmusic.com/style/honky-tonk-ma0000002648) (403 blocked; existing citation kept)
- [Handbook of Texas (TSHA): Country music](https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/country-music) — honky-tonk as a new type of country reflecting urbanization; names Tubb, Tillman, Frizzell, Price, Jones as influential; Strait's "back-to-basics honky-tonk".
- [Handbook of Texas: Ernest Tubb](https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/tubb-ernest-dale) — Tubb's electrified style "known as Texas honky-tonk" selling records in the 1940s; "Walking the Floor Over You" (1941).
- [Country Music Hall of Fame: Hank Williams](https://www.countrymusichalloffame.org/hall-of-fame/hank-williams) — "Lovesick Blues" #1 in May 1949; "Honky Tonkin'" covered for pop from 1949.
- [Country Music Hall of Fame: Lefty Frizzell](https://www.countrymusichalloffame.org/hall-of-fame/lefty-frizzell) — "burst to stardom in 1950"; sang at a Big Spring honky-tonk.
- [Wikipedia: Honky-tonk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honky-tonk) — "In the 1950s, honky-tonk entered its golden age" (Pierce, Frizzell, Jones, Williams).

## Assessment
Emergence circa 1940 and a late-1940s/1950s golden age are confirmed by TSHA, CMHOF and Wikipedia. The previous 1960 end and 1958 niche phase were removed: TSHA and CMHOF show honky-tonk as a continuing idiom (Jones, Strait). Phases are now 1940 emerging, 1948 golden-age, 1960 established. The description was rewritten with concrete context.

## Remaining uncertainty
The 1960 transition (Nashville Sound) is approximate.
