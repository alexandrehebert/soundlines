## Sources checked

- [Chuck Berry official website, biography](https://www.chuckberry.com/biography) — Artist estate biography dates his club breakthrough to joining Sir John’s Trio in 1953 and distinguishes that from his 1955 Chess signing and “Maybellene” success.
- [Songwriters Hall of Fame, Chuck Berry biography](https://www.songhall.org/profiles/chuck-berry) — Institutional biography corroborates local performances from 1950 and calls the 1953 Johnnie Johnson partnership a turning point; 1955 marks national breakthrough and recording career.
- [Rock & Roll Hall of Fame, Chuck Berry](https://rockhall.com/inductees/chuck-berry/) — Museum biography identifies his blues/R&B/country fusion and dates his broad public impact from 1955; it also records his final gig in 2014.

## Assessment

Retain 1953 as a defensible career start: the official biography and Songwriters Hall of Fame identify that year as the decisive St. Louis group/collaboration phase. Separate it from the 1955 recording and national breakthrough rather than moving the start to the first hit. 2017 is the death year; Rock Hall’s 2014 final-gig date further clarifies late performance activity. Replace the Wikipedia citation with institutional and artist-archive sources. No career phases or confidence field are present to revise.

## Remaining uncertainty

Songwriters Hall of Fame notes some paid performances began by 1950, so 1953 represents a documented local band milestone, not Berry’s first-ever professional performance.
