## Sources checked

- [The Birth of the Minimoog — Bob Moog Foundation / Moogseum](https://artsandculture.google.com/story/BwWxC5g1sP9LAA) — Foundation-curated timeline distinguishes 1969–70 prototypes, first production shipment in November 1970 and the 1972 professional catalog.
- [1972 Moog Music Catalog — Bob Moog Foundation](https://moogfoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/1972-Moog-Music-Catalog-1.pdf) — Primary company catalog presents the Minimoog as a compact instrument with named professional users, supporting the early-adoption marker.
- [Minimoog Model D Operation Manual — Moog Music](https://back.moogmusic.com/sites/default/files/2022-11/Minimoog_Model_D_Manual.pdf) — Manufacturer manual describes the portable, prewired analog Model D architecture.
- [Minimoog synthesizer — Powerhouse Collection, Museum of Applied Arts and Sciences](https://collection.powerhouse.com.au/object/72789) — Museum object record dates a Model D to 1970–72 and reports continued production throughout the 1970s.

## Assessment

The graph’s 1970 production date is supported by the Moog Foundation’s archival chronology. It now distinguishes the first shipment from prototype development and uses the surviving 1972 catalog rather than a vague popularity curve to describe early adoption.

## Remaining uncertainty

The Moog Foundation has institutional proximity to the instrument’s maker; its shipment chronology is corroborated by the Powerhouse museum record, while broad “first portable synthesizer” wording remains a manufacturer-associated claim.
