## Sources checked

- [Academic Bass Portal: 16th-Century Iconography](https://academicbassportal.com/10a/01ic/06_16/) — Organology resource places earliest double-bass specimens around the 1590s, distinguishing them from varied predecessor basses and noting their non-modern setup.
- [Vienna Symphonic Library: Double Bass](https://www.vsl.co.at/academy/strings/double-bass) — Historical overview documents overlapping “violone” terminology for bass instruments of both families and says the double bass entered opera orchestras around 1700.
- [Oxford Bibliographies in Music: Double Bass](https://academic.oup.com/reference/62400/reference-article-abstract/571523994) — Reference overview describes its mixed violin/viol family traits and orchestral role doubling cello an octave below.
- [Carnegie Hall Weill Music Institute: Double Bass](https://www.carnegiehall.org/-/media/CarnegieHall/Files/PDFs/Education/Educators/Toolbox/Grade-5/Music-of-Mexico/Toolbox-Grade-5-Music-of-Mexico-Activity-2.pdf?la=en) — Educational source identifies the double bass as the orchestra’s largest/lowest string instrument and documents use in jazz, blues, early rock and roll, bluegrass, Afro-Cuban music, and tango.
- [Double bass (Wikipedia, original graph citation)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double_bass) — Checked its discussion of violone and family-lineage debate against Oxford Bibliographies and VSL; exact ancestry remains contested.

## Assessment

The old “descended from violone” description was too categorical: reference sources document a mixed and debated lineage. Origin was shifted from circa 1550 to late-16th-century specimens around the 1590s; orchestra acceptance around 1700 is better supported than later prominence peaks.

## Remaining uncertainty

The 1590s date refers to earliest specimens leading toward the modern bass, not a settled invention date. Historical terms (violone, bass violin, double bass) were used inconsistently.
