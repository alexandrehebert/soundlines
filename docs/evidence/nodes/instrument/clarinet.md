## Sources checked

- [Clarinet in D — The Metropolitan Museum of Art](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/506724) — Museum object essay places a two-key clarinet around 1700 and says that form survived until about 1770; its attribution names J. C. Denner.
- [Clarinet — Musical Instruments Museum, Brussels](https://www.mim.be/en/collection-piece/clarinet) — Museum notes that Denner attribution rests on Doppelmayr’s 1730 account, clarifying it is historical attribution rather than a surviving signed invention record.
- [Development of the Clarinet — Northern Illinois University](https://www.niu.edu/gbarrett/resources/development.shtml) — University resource summarizes the chalumeau lineage and early dating while discussing historical uncertainty.
- [The Clarinet: From the Origins to the Family — University of North Carolina Greensboro dissertation](https://libres.uncg.edu/ir/uncg/f/Young_uncg_9520544.pdf) — Historical survey notes scholarship assigning the invention to Jacob Denner around 1710 rather than his father, supporting caution on sole attribution.

## Assessment

The broad c.1700 development date is defensible, but the evidence does not support an uncontested inventor or a popularity phase at 1770. The Met’s extant two-key instrument establishes an early form, while MIM and historical scholarship explain why attribution remains qualified.

## Remaining uncertainty

The exact transition from chalumeau to clarinet and the relative roles of Johann Christoph and Jacob Denner remain debated.
