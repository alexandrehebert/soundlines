## Sources checked

- [The Met: Curator Interview—Armadillo-Shell Charango or Jarana](https://www.metmuseum.org/es/perspectives/curator-interview-armadilloshell-charango-or-jarana) — Musical-instruments curator describes the charango as a small fretted Andean lute, notes Spanish-guitar and Indigenous adaptations, and says the smaller charango is known from the early 18th century.
- [Library of Congress: Indigenous Peoples of the Andes—Music](https://guides.loc.gov/indigenous-andes-folklife/music) — LOC identifies the charango among common Andean instruments and says Andean sound spread worldwide through 1960s Nueva Canción.
- [Duke University Musical Instrument Collections: Charango](https://sites.duke.edu/dumic/instruments/strings/central-and-south-america/charango/) — Duke collection places charango in the Andes and early 18th century and proposes Potosí/Aymara and Quechua origins; its listed references include less-specialist publications, so these origin details are treated cautiously.

## Assessment

Met offers direct curator support for the 18th-century date and hybrid construction; LOC corroborates broad Andean use and the 1960s international diffusion of Andean music. The graph’s “golden age” implied a measurable instrument-wide peak, which the evidence does not support.

## Remaining uncertainty

Specific Indigenous origin attribution and the charango’s role in Nueva Canción need stronger instrument-focused scholarship; the 1960s marker is a cautious inference from broader Andean-music dissemination.
