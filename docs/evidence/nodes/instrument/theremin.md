## Sources checked

- [RCA Theremin — The Metropolitan Museum of Art](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/739621) — Museum history dates invention to 1920 and RCA’s American production to 1929, distinguishing invention from consumer availability.
- [“A New, Perfect Musical Instrument” — Library of Congress](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/master/gdc/gdcebookspublic/20/19/66/76/29/2019667629/2019667629.pdf) — Scholarly book chapter contextualizes RCA’s 1929 mass-produced model as an early attempt to sell electronic instruments to consumers.
- [RCA Theremin — Museum of Making Music](https://museumofmakingmusic.org/momm_artifact/rca-theremin/) — Museum artifact history notes the 1928 patent, RCA licensing and the effect of the 1929 market crash on commercial prospects.

## Assessment

The 1920 origin is directly documented by the Met, with the Library of Congress and Museum of Making Music clarifying the separate 1928 patent/1929 RCA commercialization. The previous postwar “niche” phase was under-sourced and overly broad, so it was removed.

## Remaining uncertainty

The instrument’s later use spans concert performance, film, pop and experimental music; no single global popularity curve is supported by these sources.
