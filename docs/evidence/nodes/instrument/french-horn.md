## Sources checked

- [Yale University: Trompe de chasse, Vauxhall Gardens](https://artandmusic.yale.edu/handels-london/vauxhall-gardens/vauxhall-gallery6-trompe-de-chasse) — Yale states the hunting horn took its familiar hoop form in 17th-century France, was used by Lully in 1664, entered ensembles/solo repertory during the 18th century, and gained valves widely in the mid-19th century.
- [Grinnell College Musical Instrument Collection: French horn](https://omeka-s.grinnell.edu/s/MusicalInstruments/item/1535) — Existing graph citation could not be opened (HTTP 403); not relied upon for claims.

## Assessment

The existing dates are broadly supported, but the source describes separate natural-horn and valve-horn milestones. The 18th-century ensemble role is better supported than the graph’s 1800 continuation marker; phases now track documented development and ensemble adoption only.

## Remaining uncertainty

The graph’s circa-1660 origin compresses a 17th-century transition; Yale’s source dates composition by 1664 but does not establish the exact year the French hoop form first appeared.
