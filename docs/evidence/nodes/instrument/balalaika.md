## Sources checked

- [Powerhouse Collection: Balalaika](https://collection.powerhouse.com.au/object/319134) — Museum account dates the earliest documentary reference to 1688, credits Andreyev with reviving popularity in 1886 and standardizing orchestral forms, and explicitly says ancestry/origins remain debated.
- [Russian National Museum of Music: Vasily Andreyev](https://music-museum.ru/collections/expomusic/vasilij-andreev.html) — Museum biography confirms Andreyev and craftsman Franz Paserbsky developed a chromatic balalaika suitable as a concert instrument; viewed in Russian.
- [Balalaika (Wikipedia, original graph citation)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balalaika) — Earliest-reference and Andreyev claims cross-checked with Powerhouse collection and Russian National Museum of Music.

## Assessment

The exact documentary date and late-19th-century standardization are supported by a museum collection account; a national music museum corroborates the concert-instrument development. The evidence concerns documentation/revival, not continuous popularity or a measured golden age.

## Remaining uncertainty

The ancestry of the balalaika is explicitly disputed in the Powerhouse source. The Russian museum page supports concert development but does not state the same exact 1886 date.
