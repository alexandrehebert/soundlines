# The Bristol Sessions record Appalachian performers

**Review status** · reviewed

**Reviewed** · 2026-11-04

## Sources checked

- [Library of Congress, Bristol Sessions essay](https://www.loc.gov/static/programs/national-recording-preservation-board/documents/Bristol.pdf) — Dates setup to 22 July and first recording day to 25 July 1927; describes Ralph Peer’s portable Victor studio.
- [Smithsonian Folklife Festival, Appalachia: Heritage and Harmony](https://festival.si.edu/past-program/2003/appalachia-heritage-and-harmony) — Identifies the Carter Family and Jimmie Rodgers as first recorded at the sessions and calls the event foundational to the country industry, while noting it was not the first commercial country recording.
- [Library of Congress, National Recording Registry listing](https://www.loc.gov/programs/national-recording-preservation-board/recording-registry/complete-national-recording-registry-listing/) — Confirms Victor recorded 19 local musicians in Bristol in 1927.

## Assessment

July and August 1927 is appropriate. The phrase “watershed” is defensible, but avoid calling these the first country recordings: commercially distributed country material predates the sessions.
