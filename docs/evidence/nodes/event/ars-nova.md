## Sources checked

- [Oxford Academic, “Artes Novae”](https://academic.oup.com/ml/article/103/4/772/6748880) — Recent scholarly discussion places relevant Vitry-associated texts in the late 1320s or early 1330s and questions assumptions about authorship.
- [Japan Society for Aesthetics: Philippe de Vitry’s Ars nova](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/bigaku/25/3/25_KJ00003902284/_article/-char/en) — Musicological article dates the treatise conventionally called Ars nova to about 1320.
- [Fordham Medieval Sourcebook overview](https://sourcebooks.web.fordham.edu/medievalmusic.pdf) — Summarizes the conventional early-fourteenth-century chronology and notation change.

## Assessment

The earlier exact 1322 date was too precise. The treatise and attribution history is disputed; use circa 1320 as a conventional marker and describe a broader notation/practice shift, not a single invention by Vitry. One source title in this note is a Fordham Medieval Sourcebook overview.

## Remaining uncertainty

Authorship of texts associated with Ars nova and the relation between theoretical treatises and musical practice are debated.
