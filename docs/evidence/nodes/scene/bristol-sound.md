## Sources checked

- [Cambridge University Press: Just Don’t Call it Trip Hop](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/organised-sound/article/abs/just-dont-call-it-trip-hop-reconciling-the-bristol-sound-style-with-the-trip-hop-genre/B4944FEFB7C30977DA7CF0CB3AC07465) — Musicological article describes “Bristol sound” and “trip hop” as overlapping contested labels and records artists’ objections to imposed naming.
- [University of Brighton: Locating the Bristol sound](https://research.brighton.ac.uk/en/publications/locating-the-bristol-sound-archiving-music-as-everyday-life/) — Research examines venues, overlapping communities, and the challenge of documenting Bristol’s diverse music culture.
- [Victoria and Albert Museum: The Music Is Black](https://www.vam.ac.uk/articles/the-music-is-black-large-print-guide) — Exhibition material contextualizes Bristol Black music and sound-system lineages.

## Assessment

A loose, internally varied network of Bristol musicians and sound-system practitioners drawing on reggae, dub, hip-hop, punk, and electronic music. “Trip-hop” became a common outside label, while some Bristol artists preferred “Bristol sound.” Overall confidence: high.
