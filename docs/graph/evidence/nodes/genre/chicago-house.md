## Sources checked

- [Oxford University Press — The Warehouse and the Music Box: Nurturing Chicago House Music Culture](https://academic.oup.com/book/5894/chapter-abstract/149189393) — Scholarly chapter analyzes house’s emergence in queer, Black, and Latino Chicago social spaces through the Warehouse and Music Box, based on oral history and musical sources.
- [NPR Throughline / WCMU — From the Warehouse to the world](https://www.wcmu.org/music/music/2023-03-02/from-the-warehouse-to-the-world-chicago-and-the-birth-of-house-music) — Interviews with early participants describe Black and primarily gay Chicago DJs/clubs in the late 1970s–80s and Frankie Knuckles’s blend of disco, funk, electronics, and drum machines. The etymology account is presented as recollection, not settled fact.
- [The Music Origins Project — The Warehouse](https://musicorigins.org/item/the-warehouse-the-place-where-house-music-got-its-name/) — Oral-history project documents the Warehouse’s Black and Latino gay clientele and acknowledges competing accounts of the genre’s name.

## Assessment

The old 1984–1990 period and single “golden age” were too narrow for a style that developed across a continuing local and global scene. Broadened emergence to the 1980s and removed the arbitrary cutoff; kept phase confidence high only for documented scene formation.
