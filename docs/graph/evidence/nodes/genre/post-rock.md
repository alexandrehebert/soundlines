## Sources checked

- [Simon Reynolds, “Post Rock,” The Wire archive](https://www.thewire.co.uk/in-writing/book-extracts/p%3D9949) — Reynolds’s own retrospective account links the term to a network of bands and attitudes he identified in the 1990s.
- [University of Waikato research commons, post-rock genre history](https://researchcommons.waikato.ac.nz/bitstreams/1f7950b9-446f-4f13-8a26-adc624e02590/download) — Doctoral research traces the category to Reynolds’s early-1990s writing and later conceptual development.
- [Oxford Academic, post-rock book record](https://academic.oup.com/book/6294) — Academic study cross-reference supports post-rock as a contested category rather than a bounded popularity cycle.
- [AllMusic post-rock overview](https://www.allmusic.com/genre/post-rock-ma0000002790) — Specialist reference for genre characteristics; it does not establish a 2005 terminal date.

## Assessment

Changed the start from c.1991 to c.1993, the early term usage associated with Reynolds, with its meaning developed further in 1994. Removed the unsupported 2005 endpoint and prominence phases; post-rock remained an active and evolving category. Confidence: medium-high for term emergence, lower for a genre “start” because musicians and scenes preceded the label.

## Remaining uncertainty

The term’s first uses and later scope vary; the date marks its naming/conceptualization, not the first experimental music that fits it.
