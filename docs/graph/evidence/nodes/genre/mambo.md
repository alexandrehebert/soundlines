## Sources checked
- [Library of Congress National Recording Registry, Mambo No. 5](https://lcweb2.loc.gov/static/programs/national-recording-preservation-board/documents/Mambo-No-5_Firmat.pdf) — Describes the mambo’s danzón roots, Pérez Prado’s Cuban career and the Mexican recordings that propelled the style internationally.
- [David F. García, Contesting that Damned Mambo](https://www.redalyc.org/pdf/377/37716112.pdf) — Original research on early Cuban son montuno and mambo, New York’s Palladium Ballroom, and the bands of Machito and Tito Puente.

## Assessment
The 1940s mark the emergence of mambo as a distinct style, following earlier experiments in Cuba and preceding the international boom around 1950.

## Remaining uncertainty
Early accounts describe multiple Cuban antecedents, including both son montuno and danzón.
