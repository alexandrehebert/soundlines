## Sources checked

- [Oxford Academic, “The Transformation of Hardcore: DC Posthardcore, after 1983”](https://academic.oup.com/illinois-scholarship-online/book/42485/chapter-abstract/356766954) — Specialist scholarly chapter explicitly treats DC post-hardcore after 1983 and identifies bands and musical changes.
- [University of Chicago Press, Politics as Sound](https://press.uchicago.edu/ucp/books/book/chicago/P/bo51959092.html) — Academic monograph contextualizes DC hardcore 1978–83 and the transformation into later post-hardcore practices.
- [Cambridge, Popular Music Genres](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/popular-music-genres/8954EDBDDE6550E845DB5B3500B92D41) — Genre-studies overview used as background rather than evidence for a fixed endpoint.
- [AllMusic post-hardcore overview](https://www.allmusic.com/genre/post-hardcore-ma0000012125) — Specialist genre reference supports broad classification, not the old unsupported 2000 end.

## Assessment

Moved the start from 1985 to c.1983, when scholarly accounts identify a post-hardcore transformation in Washington, DC. Removed 2000 as an endpoint and the editorial golden-age/niche phases; the term encompasses later, distinct scenes and has no single end date. Confidence: medium because the name and boundaries were applied retrospectively and vary by scene.

## Remaining uncertainty

The graph uses a broad style label that may combine distinct later waves; local scenes can be represented separately if needed.
