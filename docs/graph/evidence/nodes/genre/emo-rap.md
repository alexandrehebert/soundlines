## Sources checked
- [NPR Music via KNKX, Emo rap at its limits](https://www.knkx.org/2024-04-04/emo-rap-at-its-limits) — Traces the style’s relationship to hip-hop, emo and SoundCloud and identifies Lil Peep, Juice WRLD and XXXTentacion as its breakout performers.
- [Pitchfork, Emo Rap Is Back](https://pitchfork.com/thepitch/emo-rap-is-back-here-we-go-again/) — Identifies Lil Peep, Juice WRLD and XXXTentacion as central artists of emo rap and documents its continuation.
- [Pitchfork, Come Over When You’re Sober (Part One)](https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/lil-peep-come-over-when-youre-sober-part-one/) — Documents Lil Peep’s use of emo and pop-punk samples and programmed drums.
- [TIME, Juice WRLD obituary](https://time.com/5746145/juice-wrld-dead/) — Dates his earliest online recordings to 2015 and confirms the end of his career in 2019.

## Assessment
The mid-2010s date reflects early recordings associated with the style before its mainstream breakthrough.

## Remaining uncertainty
Earlier hip-hop recordings anticipated this style. The period is an anchor for the SoundCloud generation.
