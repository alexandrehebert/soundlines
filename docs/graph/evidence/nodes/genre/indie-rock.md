## Sources checked

- [Cambridge University Press, “Indie: the Politics of Production and Distribution”](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/popular-music-genres/indie-the-politics-of-production-and-distribution/B03E618F40D47E87AF2E96C73F89316F) — Genre scholarship describes a second generation of post-punk independent labels in the early–mid 1980s.
- [Oxford University Press, “The Golden Age of Indie Rock Radio”](https://academic.oup.com/north-carolina-scholarship-online/book/61304/chapter-abstract/532701609) — University press history documents college radio’s role in 1980s indie promotion; “golden age” is specifically radio, not the whole genre.
- [Oxford University Press, “Connecting the Indie Rock Underground in the 1980s”](https://academic.oup.com/north-carolina-scholarship-online/book/61304/chapter-abstract/532692884) — Documents 1980s decentralized underground networks and college-radio curators.
- [Cambridge University Press, “Loaded”: indie guitar rock](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/popular-music/article/abs/loaded-indie-guitar-rock-canonism-white-masculinities/0FC6077711EB7596D965FF42E7B3BB7F) — Peer-reviewed article frames 1980s indie alternative rock as scene-based and autonomous, while critically examining the narrative.

## Assessment

Evidence supports a substantial 1980s indie scene and label infrastructure, but “indie” shifts between production/distribution status and sound/identity. The previous 1988 peak and 1998 niche claims were uncited editorial stages and are removed. An end date would falsely imply indie ceased after 2000.

## Remaining uncertainty

This node’s date marks the documented 1980s consolidation of the scene, not the first independent rock music; UK and US timelines differ and should not be collapsed into one precise origin.
