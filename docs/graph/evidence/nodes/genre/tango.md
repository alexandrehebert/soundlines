## Sources checked

- [Oxford Bibliographies in Music, Tango](https://academic.oup.com/reference/62400/reference-article-abstract/555404829) — Musicology reference traces origins to the Río de la Plata and its long history from late 19th century into the 21st.
- [Cambridge Companion to Tango, “A Brief History of Tango”](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/cambridge-companion-to-tango/brief-history-of-tango/46082B9A3C0529CCD1DF68474A7F7ECF) — Scholarly chapter presents African, European, Argentine and Uruguayan origins and historical changes.
- [UNESCO, Tango](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/tango-00258) — Heritage record describes a living Argentine and Uruguayan tradition developed among urban lower classes in Buenos Aires and Montevideo.
- [Library of Congress, Tango dance manual (1914)](https://www.loc.gov/item/14010455/) — Primary source documents tango’s US circulation by 1914.
- [University of Edinburgh thesis, tango origins](https://era.ed.ac.uk/bitstream/handle/1842/37328/1/Newton-Bruzza2020.pdf) — Research reviews substantial scholarly disputes over musical, cultural and social origins.

## Assessment

Kept c.1880 as a traditional approximate Rio de la Plata emergence date, now medium rather than low confidence due strong reference support, while explicitly noting scholarly disagreement. Retained 1914 as documented international circulation. No end date. The genre is a living tradition; this periodization captures one local scene and outward spread.

## Remaining uncertainty

Exact start and component influences remain debated; do not reduce tango to a single origin venue, class or national story.
