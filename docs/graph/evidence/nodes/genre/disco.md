## Sources checked

- [PBS American Experience — Sounds of the Seventies](https://www.pbs.org/wgbh/americanexperience/features/war-disco-sounds-seventies/) — Explains disco’s early-1970s blend and DJ role; reports mid-decade mainstream reach, 1977 Saturday Night Fever sales, 1979 backlash, and successor forms including house and hip-hop.
- [PBS — Disco: Soundtrack of a Revolution](https://www.pbs.org/show/disco-soundtrack-of-a-revolution/) — Documentary overview places origins in early-1970s New York marginalized communities, global peak in mid-decade, commercial backlash in 1979, and subsequent underground evolution.

## Assessment

The prior 1972 emerging / 1975 peak / 1979 established / 1980 niche / 2000 established curve was over-segmented and mislabeled 1979: authoritative histories describe 1979 as backlash and commercial decline, not consolidation. Kept broad, U.S.-focused emergence, mid-1970s mainstream high point, and early-1980s loss of mass-market dominance with underground continuation. Removed unsupported 2000 return to established. Description retained but its social origins are corroborated by Smithsonian and PBS.

## Remaining uncertainty

This curve reflects U.S. mainstream visibility and not global disco histories. Disco continued in clubs and informed house and other dance forms; there is no defensible endpoint for the broader family. Two legacy citations were inaccessible and omitted from graph sources pending later link review.
