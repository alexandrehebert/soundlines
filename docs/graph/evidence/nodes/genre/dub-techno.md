## Sources checked
- [Pitchfork, Basic Channel “BCD” (2024 Sunday review)](https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/basic-channel-bcd) — “dub techno begins and ends with Basic Channel”; 1995 BCD the “defining document”.
- [Pitchfork, BCD-2 review (2008)](https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/12081-bcd-2/) — “Basic Channel invented dub techno”; in 2008 its currency is “at its highest point in a decade” (DeepChord, Echospace).
- [The Quietus, Basic Channel 25 years on](https://thequietus.com/opinion-and-essays/anniversary/basic-channel-review-anniversary/) — nine 12-inch singles in 1993–94 moved into “reverberant dub-space”.
- [VICE, Bullshitter’s Guide to Dub Techno (2015)](https://www.vice.com/en/article/a-bullshitters-guide-to-dub-techno/) — overview of the genre and its later followers.
- [RBMA lecture: Moritz von Oswald (2008)](https://www.redbullmusicacademy.com/lectures/moritz-von-oswald-early-morning-freestyles/) — Basic Channel, then Rhythm & Sound to explore dub.
- [Inverted Audio: Deepchord interview](https://inverted-audio.com/feature/deepchord/) — “almost 20 years since Basic Channel defined an initial ‘dub techno’ aesthetic”.

## Assessment
The 1993 origin is datable to Basic Channel’s first releases in Berlin, so precision changes from decade to circa. The 2000 end date was wrong. Deepchord, Echospace and later labels continued the style, and Pitchfork described a 2008 high point. The period is now open. Phases now run emerging 1993, golden-age 1995 (BCD, Chain Reaction, Rhythm & Sound), and established from 2000.
