## Sources checked

- [Carnegie Hall, Detroit Techno timeline](https://timeline.carnegiehall.org/genres/detroit-techno) — Institutional timeline documents the Detroit origins, multiple originators and continuing scene.
- [Bentley Historical Library, Remixing Detroit History](https://bentley.umich.edu/about/collections-magazine/fall-2016/remixing-detroit-history/) — University of Michigan archive dates key Cybotron/Detroit scene activity from 1980–81 and explains local context.
- [Detroit Historical Society, Juan Atkins](https://www.detroithistorical.org/learn/online-research/encyclopedia-of-detroit/atkins-juan) — Local history documents Atkins’ role and the genre’s later international expansion.
- [Wired, “The Roots of Techno”](https://www.wired.com/1994/07/techno/) — Contemporary 1994 feature interviews Juan Atkins and documents early recordings dating from 1981.
- [Detroit Historical Society, Detroit Electronic Music Festival history](https://www.detroithistorical.org/learn/online-research/blog/flashback-2002-detroit-electronic-music-festival) — Local institution describes Cybotron’s 1983 global introduction to emerging Detroit techno.

## Assessment

Changed the mid-1980s start to c.1981, anchoring the initial Detroit electronic production and Cybotron work; the word/category “techno” gained definition later in the 1980s. Removed the arbitrary 1997 golden age and 2018 established phase. Techno spread globally and continues through distinct scenes. Confidence: medium-high for Detroit’s early scene, medium for global techno start because category naming followed initial recordings.

## Remaining uncertainty

Separate early Detroit proto-techno recordings from explicit genre naming/codification in later 1980s if the graph later supports milestone sub-events.
