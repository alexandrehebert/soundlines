## Sources checked

- [Library of Congress, Rhythm and Blues](https://www.loc.gov/collections/songs-of-america/articles-and-essays/musical-styles/popular-songs-of-the-day/rhythm-and-blues) — Institutional overview dates the term’s emergence to the 1940s and describes its original broad marketing use for African American secular music.
- [Smithsonian Folklife, “Tell It Like It Is: A History of Rhythm and Blues”](https://folklife.si.edu/magazine/freedom-sounds-tell-it-like-it-is-a-history-of-rhythm-and-blues) — Museum cultural history supports the tradition’s long, changing history across regions and musical categories.
- [Smithsonian Folkways, R&B festival program](https://folkways-media.si.edu/docs/festival/2011/festival-program-book-rnb.pdf) — Institutional overview connects late-1940s independent-label uptake and early commercial history.
- [AllMusic R&B genre overview](https://www.allmusic.com/genre/r-b-ma0000002809) — Specialist taxonomy for cross-reference; not evidence for a single 1955 golden age or 2010 establishment.

## Assessment

Retained a circa-1940s start for the term/category, not for African American musical practices that long predate it. The graph’s 1955 “golden age” and 2010 “established” phases conflate distinct historical styles (classic rhythm and blues, soul, contemporary R&B) and lack a defensible common prominence metric, so removed them. Confidence: high for the original term era; broad entry intentionally has no end.

## Remaining uncertainty

R&B has changed meaning substantially; subgenres or eras should carry their own dates if the graph needs finer chronology.
