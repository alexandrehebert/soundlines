## Sources checked

- [Cambridge, “Just Don’t Call it Trip Hop”](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/organised-sound/article/abs/just-dont-call-it-trip-hop-reconciling-the-bristol-sound-style-with-the-trip-hop-genre/B4944FEFB7C30977DA7CF0CB3AC07465) — Peer-reviewed article dates emergence to early 1990s and argues the label has been problematic, especially its conflation with Bristol Sound.
- [University of Waikato, The High Art of Trip Hop](https://researchcommons.waikato.ac.nz/entities/publication/30f44468-5a83-408c-b549-58295a43af2d) — Research distinguishes the Bristol sound from trip-hop as overlapping style and genre categories.
- [British Council, story of the Bristol Sound](https://www.britishcouncil.org.il/en/rewind-bristol) — Cultural history traces a multi-genre Bristol scene in the 1980s/90s and key 1990s releases.
- [AllMusic trip-hop overview](https://www.allmusic.com/genre/trip-hop-ma0000002906) — Specialist genre overview cross-reference.

## Assessment

Moved start from c.1993 to c.1991 for the early Bristol/downtempo scene, treating the 1994 term popularization as a separate marker. Removed 2001 end date and unsupported golden-age/established phases. Current sources emphasize label controversy and that Bristol Sound is broader/different; the artists and style continue. Confidence: medium.

## Remaining uncertainty

Keep a careful distinction between early 1990s Bristol Sound, the press-coined term trip-hop and later artists categorized under it.
