## Sources checked

- [Why Collect Heavy Metal (Canadian Association of Music Libraries journal)](https://caml.journals.yorku.ca/index.php/caml/article/download/32101/29347/33362) — Genre-history review traces early doom through late-1970s/early-1980s Sabbath-influenced bands; says term was common by mid-1980s and popularized by Candlemass’s 1986 album; notes 1990s death/doom resurgence.
- [Kahn-Harris, Extreme Metal (doctoral thesis)](https://kahn-harris.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/PhD-full-run.pdf) — Sociological study identifies doom as emerging in the 1980s and refined in the 1990s, with early examples St Vitus and The Obsessed.
- [Locating Experiential Richness in Doom Metal (UC San Diego dissertation)](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/7bq7387s) — Dissertation is focused on doom-metal history and listening; landing page was accessible but repository PDF content could not be extracted during this pass.
- [Saint Vitus Dance: The Art of Doom (Utrecht University record)](https://research-portal.uu.nl/en/publications/saint-vitus-dance-the-art-of-doom/) — Academic chapter record describes Black Sabbath as setting the doom standard; page fetch failed with cache miss.

## Assessment

Changed start from 1984 to decade-level 1980s for the distinct named genre, while the description explicitly distinguishes its early-1970s Sabbath foundations. Removed the 1997 golden-age and 2018 established phases because neither was supported by genre-level evidence; scholarship instead describes a 1990s death/doom resurgence among several later branches. No phases retained pending defensible prominence chronology.

## Remaining uncertainty

“Doom” sound precedes the genre label, and sources stress that the term’s origin is debated. Source-access failures are logged; the graph citations now emphasize sources whose relevant claims were directly checked.
