## Sources checked
- [National Endowment for the Arts, Herbie Hancock interview](https://www.arts.gov/honors/jazz/herbie-hancock) — Hancock describes combining funk rhythms and jazz improvisation on his commercially successful 1973 album Head Hunters.
- [Blue Note Records, Donald Byrd](https://www.bluenote.com/artist/donald-byrd/) — Describes Byrd’s move into electric keyboards, funk rhythms and commercially successful jazz-funk albums in the early 1970s.
- [Library of Congress, Head Hunters recording registry essay](https://www.loc.gov/static/programs/national-recording-preservation-board/documents/head-hunters.pdf) — Documents the electric keyboards and bass and the album’s fusion of funk and jazz.

## Assessment
The early 1970s were a period of wide recognition for recorded jazz-funk.

## Remaining uncertainty
The date is a timeline anchor, not a claim to the first jazz-funk recording.
