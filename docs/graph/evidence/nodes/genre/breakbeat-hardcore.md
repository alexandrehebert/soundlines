## Sources checked

- [SAGE journal article: UK rave history](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/17499755221134940) — Peer-reviewed historical/sociological study places UK rave’s rise in late 1980s and peak/notoriety in early 1990s; scene context rather than genre-specific sales evidence.
- [Rave Archive UK: 1992–1995](https://rave-archive.com/history/1992-1995/) — Specialist scene history describes fragmentation of breakbeat music and the broad “hardcore” category by 1993.
- [University of Amsterdam thesis chart](https://pure.uva.nl/ws/files/138682492/Thesis.pdf) — Research chart reports UK breakbeat/hardcore label founding activity peaking around 1992; proxy for label activity, not listener popularity.
- [British Library: Beyond the Bassline](https://www.bl.uk/about/press/releases/beyond-the-bassline-500-years-of-black-british-music-opens-at-the-british-library) — Exhibition page offers broader Black British music context; not directly used for exact dates.

## Assessment

Narrowed the graph from a 1989–open-ended sequence of six unsupported prominence labels to a circa-1990 emergence marker, one early-1990s phase and an open endpoint. The 1993 fragmentation is described as a transition, not the end of all breakbeat hardcore activity. Removed unsourced 2000/2016 revival markers and precise annual labels.

## Remaining uncertainty

Terminology overlaps with rave hardcore, old-school hardcore and proto-jungle; exact start and endpoint differ by which scene/records are included. A label-release dataset is only a proxy for prominence.

These former annotations are preserved for review context. They may be provisional or unsupported and do not assert a continuous prominence curve; the former intensity values were editorial.
