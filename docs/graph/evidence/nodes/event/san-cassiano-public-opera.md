## Sources checked

- [Cambridge Companion to Seventeenth-Century Opera, Opera for a Paying Public](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/cambridge-companion-to-seventeenthcentury-opera/opera-for-a-paying-public-italy-c-1637c-1700/14208E41A8D6F019830093DD99F97299) — Scholarly chapter identifies Venice’s Teatro San Cassiano in 1637 as the start of public opera for paying audiences.
- [Oxford University Press, Inventing the Business of Opera](https://academic.oup.com/book/32749/chapter-abstract/273089620) — Research on Venetian opera companies describes San Cassiano’s early public/commercial role.
- [Cambridge Core, Peak Period of Venetian Public Opera](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/proceedings-of-the-royal-musical-association/article/abs/peak-period-of-venetian-public-opera-the-1650s/2038EC5542D76128297C11E8354D3C36) — Musicological history documents early 1637 performances at the reopened theatre.

## Assessment

The 1637 public opera milestone is strongly supported. “Public” means ticketed paying audience, not unrestricted admission or the first opera performance; court opera predates it.
