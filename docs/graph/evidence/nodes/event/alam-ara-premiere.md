## Sources checked

- [National Film Archive of India via Google Arts & Culture](https://artsandculture.google.com/asset/alam-ara-1931/QgH9a0GoFzdsoA?hl=en) — Catalog identifies the 1931 film, its seven songs, musicians, and its status as India’s first sound film.
- [Film Heritage Foundation](https://filmheritagefoundation.co.in/alam-ara-1931-hindi-urdu-124-mins/) — Film archive documents the 14 March 1931 release and the film’s lost status.
- [Encyclopedia of Indian Cinema](https://indiancine.ma/documents/DSP/Encyclopedia%2520of%2520Indian%2520Cinema.pdf) — Reference work confirms the first sound-film release date and its music/song/dance format.

## Assessment

The 14 March 1931 release and first Indian sound-film status are well supported. “Its songs helped establish music as a central feature” is a broad historical interpretation; note it as influence, not as a separately dated invention. The film is lost, so surviving audio evidence is limited.
