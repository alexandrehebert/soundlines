## Sources checked

- [UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage: Highlife music and dance](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/highlife-music-and-dance-02141?RL=02141) — Ghana's nomination and inscription describe highlife as a living national practice; strong for the Ghana-wide tradition, not specifically Accra.
- [Cambridge University Press: “Rain or Shine We Gonna’ Rock”](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/sources-and-methods-in-african-history/rain-or-shine-we-gonna-rock-dance-subcultures-and-identity-construction-in-accra-ghana/02808FD24418BB5F63E278FA2AC22ECB) — Steven J. Salm's scholarly chapter documents highlife among popular forms shaping Accra youth subcultures in the early independence era.
- [Journal of African Arts and Culture: Indigenous Origins of Ghanaian Highlife Music](https://journals.uew.edu.gh/index.php/jaac/article/view/507) — Ghanaian academic article describes Accra dance orchestras and halls alongside other Ghanaian centers; its account cautions against treating the city as the sole origin.

## Assessment

The city-to-highlife connection is supported. UNESCO is national in scope, while the Cambridge chapter and Ghanaian music scholarship provide the needed city-level context. Overall confidence: medium, because the entry makes a broad cultural-centrality claim rather than a narrowly dated fact.
