## Sources checked

- [Phyllis Martin, *Leisure and Society in Colonial Brazzaville* (Cambridge University Press)](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/leisure-and-society-in-colonial-brazzaville/78838195EC9177E98BE8896A162A2123) — Scholarly urban history documents local music, dance, and nightlife.
- [UNESCO Courier: Congolese rumba](https://courier.unesco.org/fr/articles/nouvelle-jeunesse-pour-la-rumba-congolaise) — Describes rumba as shared by the two Congos and performed in cities on both sides of the river.
- [University of Edinburgh, *Rumba from Congo to Cape Town*](https://era.ed.ac.uk/server/api/core/bitstreams/a182425b-72e1-4149-86db-df594f777378/content) — Research explicitly traces Congolese rumba’s beginnings to both Brazzaville and Léopoldville/Kinshasa, while distinguishing Kinshasa’s later continental reach.

## Assessment

Brazzaville is a key contributor, but the genre’s history is transnational across the river and must not be attributed to this city alone. Overall confidence: medium-high.
