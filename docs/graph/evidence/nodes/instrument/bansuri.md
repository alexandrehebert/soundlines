## Sources checked

- [Carl Clements, “Tradition and Innovation in the Bānsurī Performance Style of Pannalal Ghosh,” IAWM Journal](https://journal.iftawm.org/previous/vol1no2/clements/) — Scholarly article credits Ghosh with popularizing bansuri in modern Hindustani classical music and says he adapted it for the North Indian classical stage in the 1930s–40s; distinguishes this from India’s much older transverse-flute tradition.
- [Smithsonian Folkways: Venu](https://folkways.si.edu/hariprasad-zakir-hussain/venu/world/music/album/smithsonian) — Identifies bansuri as the bamboo flute used by Hindustani classical instrumentalist Hariprasad Chaurasia; recording made live in 1974, released 1989.
- [Grinnell College Musical Instrument Collection: Bansuri](https://omeka-s.grinnell.edu/s/MusicalInstruments/item/2108) — Existing citation could not be opened (HTTP 403); not treated as independently checked.
- [DMI Virtual Instrument: Bansuri research](https://dimultimusic.co.id/vst/research/bansuri/) — Accessible synthesis distinguishes long flute history from 20th-century classical-concert entry through Pannalal Ghosh and transparently cites sources; used as secondary cross-check, not sole authority.

## Assessment

The original graph blurred the bamboo flute’s old South Asian history with its newer Hindustani concert role. Clements supports the 1930s–40s modern concert development; Smithsonian Folkways confirms later performance by a leading Hindustani artist. Removed the unsupported 1960 peak and weakened 1980 transition confidence.

## Remaining uncertainty

The precise decade when the bansuri became broadly established in Hindustani classical music is not established by the sources checked; 1980 is only an approximate marker inferred from a 1974 recording and 1989 release.
