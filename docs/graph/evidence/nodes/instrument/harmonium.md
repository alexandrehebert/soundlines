## Sources checked

- [Smithsonian Folkways, Indian Harmonium](https://folkways.si.edu/indian-harmonium/music/playlist/smithsonian) — Describes one-hand bellows/keyboard form and vocal accompaniment in India from the 20th century; notes initial criticism and later acceptance.
- [Smithsonian Folkways, Pakistan: The Music of the Qawal](https://folkways.si.edu/pakistan-the-music-of-the-qawal/world/music/album/smithsonian) — Documents a qawwali ensemble accompanying singers on small, hand-pumped portable harmoniums; 1977 UNESCO Collection release.
- [Grinnell College Musical Instrument Collection, harmonium](https://omeka-s.grinnell.edu/s/MusicalInstruments/item/1526) — Search extract states 1842 patent and mid-19th-century missionary introduction to India; page itself blocked (403).
- [Matthew Rahaim, “That Ban(e) of Indian Music”](https://www.ocf.berkeley.edu/~mrahaim/that%20ban%28e%29%20of%20indian%20music.pdf) — Search extract from scholarly article ties Debain patent to 1842 and discusses spread in India; direct PDF fetch unavailable.

## Assessment

The graph’s 1840 circa date conflated harmonium development with patent records. 1842 is the best-supported patent date in the checked references. Folkways sources corroborate South Asian vocal accompaniment and a specific qawwali ensemble, but do not support an 1880 golden-age claim.

## Remaining uncertainty

The exact timing and regional sequence of adoption in South Asia need a full-text scholarly source; the 1900 phase is deliberately coarse.
