## Sources checked

- [Bate Collection, University of Oxford: Bassoons](https://www.bate.ox.ac.uk/bassoons) — Museum collection history identifies the curtal as predecessor, development during the Baroque, and key additions around 1650; its earliest surviving collection bassoon is c.1720.
- [Victoria and Albert Museum: Catalogue of Wind Instruments](https://www.vam.ac.uk/__data/assets/pdf_file/0011/178499/Catalogue_Wind_Instruments.pdf) — Search-indexed catalogue text describes the bassoon as a four-jointed curtal/dulcian modification introduced in the second half of the 17th century; the PDF URL returned 404 on open.
- [The Met: Bassoon by Wolfgang Thomae](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/503660) — Met dates this example to c.1750 and says bassoon served as the bass of double-reed consorts and was important in Baroque continuo.

## Assessment

Oxford and V&A corroborate a second-half-17th-century jointed bassoon deriving from curtal/dulcian; Met confirms its Baroque ensemble role. The original transition labels claimed more than these sources establish, so I retained only emergence and documented Baroque establishment.

## Remaining uncertainty

V&A catalogue file is currently unavailable at its cited URL; its relevant text was available only in search indexing. The exact date/priority of the four-jointed design remains approximate.
