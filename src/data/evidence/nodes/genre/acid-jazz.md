## Sources checked

- [Jazz for the iPod Generation: Digital Media and Jazz in the UK (University of Salford thesis)](https://salford-repository.worktribe.com/OutputFile/1497243) — Academic thesis documents 1980s British jazz-funk/soul club culture, Gilles Peterson’s use of “acid jazz” in 1987, acid jazz as a marketing/musical revival, and continued success of key bands through the 1990s.
- [Acid Jazz Records — About the label](https://acidjazz.co.uk/pages/about-acid-jazz) — Label’s own history says Eddie Piller founded it to document a small London scene; describes the label’s role in the international scene, key artists and ongoing releases. First-party perspective, used for continuity, not neutral chronology.
- [WDET — The Progressive Underground’s Primer to Acid Jazz](https://wdet.org/2020/08/28/the-progressive-undergrounds-primer-to-acid-jazz/) — Station history describes the mid-1980s London club roots and says the Acid Jazz label began in 1988; useful conflicting year for label naming/founding versus Peterson’s 1987 term use.
- [Official Charts — Jamiroquai](https://www.officialcharts.com/artist/28264/jamiroquai/) — UK chart history identifies the band with acid jazz and documents its 1990s commercial reach, including multiple Top 10 singles/albums and the 1998 No.1 “Deeper Underground.” These hits evidence artist success, not a genre-wide peak.
- [James Taylor Quartet official biography (legacy graph citation)](https://www.jamestaylorquartet.co.uk/jtq/biography/) — Official site loaded without readable biography text in this fetch; removed as an unchecked source rather than relying on its claims.

## Assessment

The previous 1997 end date and 1995 niche phase were not supported: scholarship and the label’s own current history show continued activity, while prominent acid-jazz-associated acts remained commercially successful into and beyond the 1990s. Removed endpoint and all prominence phases because existing evidence was artist-level/loosely editorial. Kept a circa-1987 start, distinguishing Peterson’s term use from the label’s 1988 account of its founding. Replaced Wikipedia and AllMusic with academic research, the originating label, a station history and chart data.

## Remaining uncertainty

Acid jazz can refer to a musical tendency, the London club scene, the label, or its roster. Source accounts differ on whether the term arose in 1987 or the label formally coined/promoted it in 1988. There is no supported end date.
