## Sources checked

- [Evans, “The Art of Capping,” Cambridge Companion to Global Rap](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/cambridge-companion-to-global-rap/art-of-capping/1767EE7D51C7CA43FBB5B8B57EA9865C) — Peer-reviewed Cambridge chapter explicitly locates drill’s birth in Chicago underground hip-hop in the early 2010s and studies artists’ platform practices.
- [Ilan, “Digital Street Culture” (City St George’s repository)](https://openaccess.city.ac.uk/id/eprint/23519/1/DigitalStreetCultureAcceptedVersion.pdf) — Peer-reviewed cultural criminology article dates London UK drill proliferation to mid-to-late 2010s, identifies Chicago influence, and cautions against reading lyrics/video as literal evidence.
- [Levey, The Inward Turn of Chicago Drill Rap (Northwestern University repository)](https://arch.library.northwestern.edu/concern/generic_works/sb397830m) — University research situates Chicago drill within local street life and its national mainstream visibility through Chief Keef; supports the scene context, not a global chronology.

## Assessment

The 2010s decade start is supported. Removed the graph’s 2015 “golden-age” and 2018 “established” phase claims because they were not supported as genre-wide prominence markers. Expanded description to distinguish Chicago origins from later UK development; kept no endpoint because regional scenes continue and evolve. Replaced AllMusic-only sourcing with specialist scholarship.

## Remaining uncertainty

Chicago drill and UK drill should not be treated as identical; this general entry gives the lineage but local histories need individual genre entries. Sources document emergence and spread, not a defensible golden-age or decline curve.
