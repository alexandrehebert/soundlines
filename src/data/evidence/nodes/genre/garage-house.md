## Sources checked

- [Oxford Handbook chapter on queer dance spaces](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9780199793631_A42856066/preview-9780199793631_A42856066.pdf) — Academic account describes the Paradise Garage community and Levan’s sound as a precursor, parallel or substyle of house in retrospective classification.
- [Routledge dance-music history chapter](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9781136783173_A29485819/preview-9781136783173_A29485819.pdf) — Scholarly history says garage took shape as a distinct genre after the club closed in late 1987.
- [Duke University Press scholarly chapter](https://assets-us-01.kc-usercontent.com/f7ca9afb-82c2-002a-a423-84e111d5b498/b183102d-8504-4fb4-a0c2-d1cfdcdeedd3/978-1-4780-2504-7_601.pdf?auto=format&fm=webp&lossless=true) — Describes garage house as a later name for a sound developed at Paradise Garage, rooted in disco, funk, soul and early Chicago house.

## Assessment

The previous 1982–1990 period conflated club context with a bounded genre life. Sources make clear the “garage” sound was associated with Paradise Garage, but classification as a distinct genre was retrospective and may postdate the 1987 club closure. Removed the false endpoint and “golden age” label; phase marks later consolidation, not the venue’s beginning.

## Remaining uncertainty

There is no consensus origin date because sources variously mean Levan’s DJ sound, club culture, records, or genre label. A sourceable future representation could split the venue/scene from garage-house genre.
