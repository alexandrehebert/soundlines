## Sources checked

- [Till, “Chill Out: Seeking Ecstatic Trance in Low-Tempo EDM” (Cambridge Companion to Electronic Dance Music)](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/cambridge-companion-to-electronic-dance-music/chill-out/56E251BCE9BAD5EF081F738AD3336156) — Peer-reviewed chapter says downtempo EDMC genres were popularized from 1989, have earlier roots, and names ambient, ambient dub, ambient techno, chill-out, downtempo, ambient house, chill hop and trip hop as variants.
- [University of Huddersfield research record for Till chapter](https://pure.hud.ac.uk/en/publications/chill-out-seeking-ecstatic-trance-in-low-tempo-edm/) — Institutional record independently reproduces the chapter abstract and confirms the 1989 popularization and umbrella scope.
- [Brett, Turn On, Tune In, Drift Off (Oxford Academic)](https://academic.oup.com/book/44623/chapter-abstract/378621525) — Scholarly book chapter abstract documents ambient’s popularization from late 1980s to mid-1990s as a related EDM strand, supporting distinct constituent histories rather than one unified downtempo arc.
- [AllMusic — Downbeat overview (legacy graph citation)](https://www.allmusic.com/style/downbeat-ma0000012193) — Graph’s prior URL does not match AllMusic’s current Downbeat entry; excluded rather than treating a neighboring term as direct downtempo authority.

## Assessment

The prior end date 2005 and emerging/golden-age/niche phases were unsupported and too tidy for a broad umbrella. Cambridge scholarship supports circa-1989 popularization but explicitly recognizes earlier roots and multiple related styles. Removed endpoint and phases instead of implying a single peak/decline curve. Corrected the AllMusic mismatch: its cited URL was for a different downbeat page.

## Remaining uncertainty

Downtempo is unstable as a genre label and varies by scene and market; 1989 dates popularization of the family, not the first low-tempo electronic music. This entry cannot defensibly carry a common end date or prominence arc.
