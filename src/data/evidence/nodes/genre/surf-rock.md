## Sources checked

- [Washington Post, surf-rock early history](https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/entertainment/1982/08/15/c00eadbf-4268-4701-b3b4-d34ba5cf0e46/) — Retrospective article describes Dick Dale’s 1961 instrumental as inaugurating regional surf music and ensuing California/national craze.
- [Los Angeles Times, Dick Dale history](https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1992-01-30-ol-1582-story.html) — Documents 1961 “Let’s Go Trippin’” as his first hit and surf-rock milestone.
- [Edinburgh doctoral thesis on surf-rock imagery and music](https://era.ed.ac.uk/bitstream/handle/1842/7954/Sanchez2012.pdf) — Research source analyzes Dale, recording and reverb-centered sound and the early scene.
- [AllMusic surf overview](https://www.allmusic.com/style/surf-ma0000002883) — Specialist reference for category definition; not a justification for a 1965 end date.

## Assessment

Moved the broad start from c.1960 to c.1959 as an approximate scene origin; retained 1961 as a source-backed popularization milestone. The former 1965 endpoint confused an early surf craze’s decline with the continuing style and later revivals. Confidence: medium; precursor instrumental rock exists and “surf” as a scene/style was not instant or singular.

## Remaining uncertainty

An exact first surf-rock recording remains debatable; this period represents the Southern California scene, not every surf-influenced instrumental.
