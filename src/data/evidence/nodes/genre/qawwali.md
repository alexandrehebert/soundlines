## Sources checked

- [Fordham University Press, Qawwali study chapter](https://academic.oup.com/fordham-scholarship-online/book/24268/chapter-abstract/185921803) — Scholarly book chapter on devotional music provides context for the living South Asian Sufi performance tradition.
- [Delhi University, Delhi through the Ages](https://www.du.ac.in/uploads/new-web/29122022_Appendix-109.pdf) — Historical chapter distinguishes Khusrau-era antecedents from a composite qawwali tradition named in Shahjahanabad in the 18th century.
- [King’s College London, Amir Khusrau’s musical legacy dissertation](https://kclpure.kcl.ac.uk/ws/portalfiles/portal/42286233/2004BrownSchofieldPhD.pdf) — Musicological research supports historical links between Khusrau and Delhi qawwali musicians while challenging simplistic origin attributions.
- [AllMusic qawwali overview](https://www.allmusic.com/genre/qawwali-ma0000002742) — Specialist taxonomy cross-reference; not treated as proof that the genre was fully formed in 1300.

## Assessment

Kept c.1300 only as a conventional origin marker for Khusrau-era devotional antecedents, not a precise formation year. Research also indicates qawwali as a composite named performance tradition took shape later, including an 18th-century account; the Khusrau attribution is historically important but cannot be stated as a settled single inventor. Removed the “established” phase at the start. Confidence: medium-low for a single origin date, high for living tradition and broad lineage.

## Remaining uncertainty

Origin, naming and consolidation refer to different milestones and should remain distinguished; source traditions and retrospective attributions vary.
