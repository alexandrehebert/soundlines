## Sources checked

- [AllMusic, pop-punk overview](https://www.allmusic.com/style/pop-punk-ma0000004449) — Specialist taxonomy distinguishes late-1970s antecedents from the 1994 Green Day/Weezer mainstream wave.
- [Oxford Handbook of Punk Rock](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/38573) — Scholarly handbook situates punk’s late-1970s context and later international/recurrent history.
- [TIME, article on Tom Petty’s 1977 use/criticism of pop-punk term](https://www.theatlantic.com/magazine/2026/06/springsteen-petty-politics-osmon/686936/?utm_source=apple_news) — Music journalism notes the label was used in 1977 for Tom Petty and the Heartbreakers, illustrating the term’s earlier, broader use.
- [Cambridge, Popular Music Genres](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/popular-music-genres/contents/11B809B0781E7EEB743711DF46B7DE4B) — Academic genre handbook for punk context; does not warrant an end date of 2005.

## Assessment

Kept c.1977 as the earliest label/precursor milestone, but documented that this early use did not yet mean the later, more specific Green Day-style subgenre. AllMusic places an explicit popularization wave in 1994. Removed the unsupported 2005 endpoint and speculative 1988/2003 prominence phases; retained the sourced 1994 milestone in the phase note. Confidence: medium because the label’s meaning shifts substantially across periods.

## Remaining uncertainty

The graph should distinguish early punk-pop description from the 1990s pop-punk category if later entries require greater precision.
