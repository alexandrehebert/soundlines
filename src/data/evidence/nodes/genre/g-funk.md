## Sources checked

- [The Chronic — Library of Congress National Recording Registry](https://lcweb2.loc.gov/static/programs/national-recording-preservation-board/documents/the-chronic.pdf) — Registry essay calls “Nuthin’ But a ‘G’ Thang” a blueprint for G-funk and credits The Chronic with bringing the subgenre into the mainstream; notes Cold 187um’s originator claim.
- [Historical Accounts of the Origins of Hip-Hop — University of Nottingham dissertation](https://eprints.nottingham.ac.uk/11081/1/JustinWilliams_PhDfinal.pdf) — Doctoral history describes a 1992–96 G-funk era and situates the sound within competing West Coast rap histories.
- [Gangster Boogie: Los Angeles and the Rise of Gangsta Rap, 1965–1992 — UC eScholarship](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/3hd2d12n) — Scholarly dissertation contextualizes Los Angeles production and the G-funk breakthrough around The Chronic; supports a scene-based account rather than a sole-inventor claim.
- [“Backstabbing, Moogs and the funky worm” — The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/music/2016/sep/13/gangsta-rap-history-nwa-ice-cube-dr-dre) — History of the Los Angeles scene records Above the Law’s competing claim to the G-funk sound.

## Assessment

The prior 1998 endpoint implied the style stopped, and an AllMusic category alone did not support exact phases. The Library of Congress and scholarly histories support a strong early-1990s commercial wave, while the origin is contested. Removed the end date and retained a 1992 breakthrough marker.

## Remaining uncertainty

The label’s origin is contested, and “G-funk era” often denotes a commercial prominence window rather than a cleanly bounded musical style.
