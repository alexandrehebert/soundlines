## Sources checked

- [Oxford Academic: Heaviness in Metal Music, Introduction](https://academic.oup.com/book/62310/chapter/552773754) — Scholarly introduction argues against naming one first metal band and emphasizes longer pre-metal histories.
- [Oxford Academic: “Leaving the Blues Behind”](https://academic.oup.com/book/62310/chapter/552774038) — Chapter contextualizes late-1960s heaviness in Black blues traditions and racialized historiography.
- [Routledge: Black Sabbath and the Rise of Heavy Metal](https://www.routledge.com/Black-Sabbath-and-the-Rise-of-Heavy-Metal-Music/Cope/p/book/9781138254138) — Scholarly monograph traces metal’s evolution and development in late-1960s/early-1970s Birmingham.
- [Library of Congress-hosted scholarly volume](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/master/gdc/gdcebookspublic/20/20/71/81/47/2020718147/2020718147.pdf) — Academic chapter warns that no agreed origin supports a simple linear origin narrative.

## Assessment

Late-1960s roots are defensible, but date should be approximate and historiographically cautious: Oxford scholarship underscores racialized antecedents and disputes over which bands qualify. Removed unsupported 1983 “golden age” and 2010 “established” phase; the genre remains active and fragmented.

## Remaining uncertainty

1968 is a broad marker for the evolution of heavy rock toward metal, not an uncontested birth year or claim of a single inventor.
