## Sources checked

- [Partridge, “King Tubby Meets the Upsetter at the Grass Roots of Dub” (Popular Music History)](https://research.lancaster-university.uk/en/publications/king-tubby-meets-the-upsetter-at-the-grass-roots-of-dub-some-thou/) — Peer-reviewed history locates dub in Jamaican ska/reggae sound-system culture and defines it as studio deconstruction; it emphasizes a complex, collective genesis rather than one uncontested inventor.
- [Vendryes, “Versions, Dubs and Riddims” (Dancecult)](https://dj.dancecult.net/index.php/dancecult/article/download/678/692/2690) — Peer-reviewed article distinguishes late-1960s versioning from dub as a distinct early-1970s genre; says it flourished through the 1970s, receded in Jamaica mid-1980s as techniques entered dancehall, and continued internationally.
- [Veal, Dub: Soundscapes and Shattered Songs in Jamaican Reggae (Wesleyan University Press)](https://www.weslpress.org/9780819574428/dub/) — Ethnomusicologist’s award-winning monograph dates dub music to early 1970s and explains studio technology as an instrument; notes broad influence beyond Jamaica.
- [University of Liverpool thesis, Dub in Babylon](https://livrepository.liverpool.ac.uk/3174425/) — Research thesis distinguishes the remix technique’s late-1960s roots from dub’s later genre identity and discusses British punk/post-punk reception.
- [Jamaica Information Service — Dub (legacy graph candidate)](https://jis.gov.jm/jamaica/dub/) — Government source search excerpt states producers/engineers pioneered dub beginning in the late 1960s; direct page fetch timed out, so not retained in graph sources.

## Assessment

Corrected the prior 1968 start: sources distinguish late-1960s instrumental/version practices from dub as a distinct genre that emerged in the early 1970s. Set decade-level 1970 start, kept open-ended because dub techniques and diaspora scenes persist, and removed unsupported 1983 golden-age/2010 established phases. Description now distinguishes production practice from genre and avoids a single-inventor claim.

## Remaining uncertainty

Sources give slightly different boundaries because “dub” can denote a production technique, Jamaican genre, or global diaspora style. The 1980s decline refers to autonomous popularity in Jamaica, not disappearance elsewhere; no overall end date or simple phases fit.
