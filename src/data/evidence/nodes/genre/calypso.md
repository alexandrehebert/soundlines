## Sources checked

- [National Library and Information System Authority of Trinidad and Tobago — Calypso](https://www.nalis.gov.tt/portfolio-item/calypso/) — National library account says the art form predates the term, which Errol Hill dates to 1900; it also identifies 1914 as the first calypso recording and describes the tradition’s Carnival and post-emancipation roots. Search-result extract checked; page fetch returned 502.
- [University of Trinidad and Tobago — Recognition of Calypso Music on an International Arena](https://utt.edu.tt/index.php?article_key=8782&articles=1&show_archive=1&wk=1) — University account explicitly says calypso history predates the 1900s.
- [Oxford Scholarship — The Trinidadian Calypso to 1940](https://doi.org/10.14325/mississippi/9781496806895.003.0002) — Scholarly chapter provides a dedicated historical account of Trinidadian calypso through 1940; citation supports its pre-recording-history scope.
- [University of the West Indies journal — Creole and Post-Creole: The Music of Carnival](https://journals.sta.uwi.edu/ojs/index.php/cd/article/view/79) — Scholarly article characterizes recognizable Trinidad calypso as emerging through a complex Carnival folk process.

## Assessment

Removed the graph’s 1900 origin, 1960 end, and unsupported 1912/1948/1958 prominence phases. Sources distinguish the late-documented name and recording industry from the much older, partly oral Carnival song tradition; a precise global inception year is not justified.
