## Sources checked

- [UNESCO, “Raï, popular folk song of Algeria”](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/rai-popular-folk-song-of-algeria-01894) — UNESCO describes the Algerian folk-song tradition, early twentieth-century female singers, and its later national and international circulation.
- [bpb, Raï](https://www.bpb.de/kurz-knapp/lexika/islam-lexikon/21636/rai/) — German Federal Agency for Civic Education dates Oran instrumentation changes to the 1920s and internationally popular modern raï to the late 1970s.
- [Cambridge University Press, “Seeing Music in Early Twentieth-Century Colonial Algeria”](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/twentieth-century-music/article/seeing-music-in-early-twentieth-century-colonial-algeria/70682F4BC3DF1BFB5B6438BFDFABD591) — Peer-reviewed historical study situates raï and cheikhat within early twentieth-century western Algerian public music culture and later European circulation.

## Assessment

The tradition predates the modern pop form; 1920s is a defensible marker for changes in Oran, not the absolute origin. The late-1970s international pop form is separately documented. Removed unsupported 1980 and 2000 stages and replaced Wikipedia with UNESCO and scholarship. Confidence: high for broad milestones; medium for exact inception.

## Remaining uncertainty

Traditional raï roots and the modern internationally popular form are related but distinct historical phases; avoid collapsing them into a single origin date.
