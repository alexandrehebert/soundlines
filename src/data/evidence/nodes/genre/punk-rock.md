## Sources checked

- [Oxford Handbook of Punk Rock](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/38573) — Scholarly reference treats proto-punk antecedents, an approximately 1976–84 high period, and later international/continuing history.
- [“Punk’s Origins: Anglo-American Syncretism,” Journal of Intercultural Studies](https://doi.org/10.1080/0725686032000165388) — Academic article challenges single-country origin stories and traces cross-Atlantic formation in the mid-to-late 1970s.
- [University of California Press, “Defining Punk: Queerness and the LA Punk Scene, 1977–1981”](https://online.ucpress.edu/jpms/article-abstract/30/1-2/155/106017/Defining-PunkQueerness-and-the-LA-Punk-Scene-1977) — Peer-reviewed article documents a specific local scene, reinforcing that punk developed through multiple settings.
- [University of Adelaide library, punk scholarship overview](https://digital.library.adelaide.edu.au/server/api/core/bitstreams/bc316e22-891c-40f1-8fde-8f07c5d361d9/content) — Academic literature review notes punk’s 1970s origins and historiographic development.

## Assessment

Kept c.1974 as an approximate precursor/formation marker for punk rock while removing the false 1980 endpoint; scholarship explicitly tracks later waves and ongoing scenes. Removed the lone golden-age phase since it labeled the same short original movement as if the whole genre ended. Confidence: medium; the graph’s start spans proto-punk and the later recognized movement, which are not identical.

## Remaining uncertainty

If a tighter definition is desired, separate proto-punk/early punk antecedents from the mid-1970s punk movement rather than forcing one global origin date.
