## Sources checked

- [Britannica, bebop](https://www.britannica.com/art/bebop) — “first kind of modern jazz”; split jazz “in the last half of the 1940s”; originated early 1940s with Gillespie, Christian, Monk, Clarke, Parker; hard bop a later style.
- [Carnegie Hall timeline, bebop](https://timeline.carnegiehall.org/genres/bebop) — “evolved during the 1940s”; Minton’s jam sessions; Gillespie, Parker, Monk, Powell; “Koko”, “Shaw ’Nuff” (1945).
- [Wikipedia, bebop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bebop) — page retrieved; consistent early-1940s origin; retained as orientation.

## Assessment

Period start circa 1940 and description confirmed by two independent institutional sources. Phases revised: golden age moved from 1948 to 1945 (the 1945 Parker–Gillespie recordings and the late-1940s split Britannica describes); the late “niche” phase replaced by an established phase from 1951, since bop became the common modern-jazz vocabulary rather than vanishing. The 1960 end is kept as the end of bebop as the leading style. Britannica and Carnegie Hall added as sources; phase confidence recorded.

## Remaining uncertainty

Exact peak/decline boundaries are editorial; no source quantifies prominence.
