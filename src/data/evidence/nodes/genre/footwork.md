## Sources checked

- [Geometry of a Ghost: Chicago Footwork and the Sound System Continuum — Yale University](https://elischolar.library.yale.edu/gsas_dissertations/1375/) — Dissertation describes footwork as Black electronic music/dance rooted in 1980s Chicago house and provides a cultural history of the form.
- [Soulcraft: Theorizing Black Techne in African and American Viral Dance — Social Media + Society](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/20563051221107644) — Peer-reviewed ethnographic article dates footwork’s emergence out of house battle dancing in the 1990s and global reach to 2007.
- [The Evolution of Footwork — Resident Advisor](https://ra.co/features/1235) — Chicago scene history explains the local relationship among juke, dance crews and footwork; places an important transition in early-2000s local club culture.
- [Teklife / Ghettoville / Eski — Goldsmiths, University of London](https://research.gold.ac.uk/id/eprint/28833/7/9781912685790_Teklife_Book.pdf) — Academic book traces Chicago footwork and the Teklife network, emphasizing local contributors and DJ Rashad’s role in international recognition.

## Assessment

The original 2001 “golden age” and 2018 established marker were not supported. Research links the dance to 1990s Chicago battle culture and describes international visibility by 2007, while its roots extend to 1980s house. Kept the local juke/footwork distinction explicit.

## Remaining uncertainty

Local terminology and chronology vary by neighborhood and practitioner; 1990s and 2007 are broad scene/visibility anchors, not a definitive genre birth or popularity curve.
