## Sources checked

- [Popular Music and Society: “Ground Zero” of Goth](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/03007766.2010.537928) — Peer-reviewed article treats 1979 “Bela Lugosi’s Dead” as putative—not undisputed—origin.
- [Kingston University research on goth music](https://eprints.kingston.ac.uk/43293/1/Bannister-C-R.pdf) — Academic article critiques repetitive origin stories centered on the 1979 track.
- [Duke University Press: Goth: Undead Subculture](https://www.dukeupress.edu/goth) — Scholarly collection frames goth as emerging from late-1970s British punk and continually reinventing itself.

## Assessment

The 1979 anchor is well established in retrospective histories but the peer-reviewed literature explicitly calls it a putative origin and questions its singularity. The earlier end date and 1988 niche classification have no sufficient support and are removed. The description’s distinction between gothic rock and goth subculture remains useful.

## Remaining uncertainty

A fuller genre history should treat multiple bands and the scene’s gradual consolidation, not assume one recording created it.
