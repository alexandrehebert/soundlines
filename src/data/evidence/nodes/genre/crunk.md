## Sources checked

- [Southern Spaces, “Dirty Decade: Rap Music and the US South, 1997–2007”](https://southernspaces.org/2008/dirty-decade-rap-music-and-us-south-1997-2007/) — Scholarly historical essay treats crunk as a contested style and regional identity, traces its earlier Southern rap roots, and describes its shift from underground club music to mainstream visibility.
- [NPR Music, Southern rap 2000–2004](https://www.wprl.org/npr-music/npr-music/2020-08-03/the-south-got-something-to-say-a-celebration-of-southern-rap-2000-2004) — Regina N. Bradley, PhD, contextualizes Lil Jon’s 1997 regional success and Kings of Crunk as establishing national subgenre prominence; notes chanting, hard-hitting bass, and Memphis/Atlanta roots.
- [Red Bull Music Academy Daily, “Lil Jon, ‘Yeah!’ and the Evolution of Crunk”](https://daily.redbullmusicacademy.com/2019/09/lil-jon-usher-ludacris-yeah/) — Documents 2002 Kings of Crunk, early-2000s crossover, a later shift toward snap and trap, and the continued diversification rather than disappearance of crunk’s sound.
- [Oxford Handbook of Hip Hop Music, “On Politics and Performativity in Atlanta Hip Hop Party Culture”](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/34741/chapter-abstract/296545147) — Search abstract identifies crunk as an Atlanta-based hip-hop subgenre and discusses chant-based performance; full page fetch was blocked by the publisher, so not counted as full-text review.

## Assessment

Removed the unsupported 2008 endpoint and unsupported 2006 “niche” transition. Southern Spaces describes crunk’s older regional club roots and its contested identity; NPR documents the 2002–04 national rise; Red Bull documents the later transition to snap/trap while emphasizing that crunk did not disappear. Broadened the start to the 1990s with decade precision and low confidence, rather than implying an exact origin year. Replaced the generic AllMusic artist pages with scholarship and contextual histories.

## Remaining uncertainty

Crunk is a contested style/identity rather than a uniformly bounded genre. The 1990s start and 2005 phase transition are approximate editorial markers; regional roots and later influence predate and outlast mainstream labeling. Oxford chapter full text could not be retrieved.
