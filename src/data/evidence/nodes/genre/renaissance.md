## Sources checked

- [Oxford Bibliographies in Music, Renaissance](https://academic.oup.com/reference/62400/reference-article-abstract/555391104) — Musicology reference defines the Western art music period as approximately 1430–1600 and explains stylistic criteria and boundary debate.
- [Princeton University, Renaissance Music research guide](https://libguides.princeton.edu/renaissance-music) — University guide summarizes musicological resources and uses roughly 1430–1600.
- [Metropolitan Museum of Art, Music in the Renaissance](https://www.metmuseum.org/pt/essays/music-in-the-renaissance) — Museum essay discusses European music within the broader 1400–1600 cultural period, illustrating why a wider conventional frame persists.
- [Oxford Academic, “Was there a Renaissance in Music?”](https://academic.oup.com/book/47646/chapter-abstract/422358004) — Musicological chapter says most scholars use c.1430–1600 but emphasizes disagreement and critiques the period label.

## Assessment

Narrowed the start from c.1400 to c.1430 for the Western art music period, following musicological reference criteria; retained the approximate 1600 transition. Some cultural histories use 1400–1600, so the earlier date is not universally wrong but too broad for this musical period definition. Removed four arbitrary prominence stages; this is a stylistic historical period, not a popularity curve. Confidence: high for conventional musicology range, with acknowledged boundaries debate.

## Remaining uncertainty

The entry currently refers specifically to Western art music; other global traditions in the same centuries are not captured by “Renaissance music.”
