## Sources checked

- [Wayne Marshall et al., “Oye mi canto: The History and Politics of Reggaetón,” Popular Music and Society](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/03007760802218046) — Peer-reviewed article outlines early history and defining features and centers Puerto Rican race/youth context.
- [UC eScholarship, “Orgulloso de mi Caserío”: Reggaetón dissertation](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/09d1f6q0) — Archival/ethnographic research identifies underground precursor in mid-1990s Puerto Rico.
- [Library of Congress, Nación Reggaetón](https://www.loc.gov/item/hlas-bi2013001267/) — Library catalog cross-reference records scholarly treatment of music history in Puerto Rico.
- [TIME, Loud podcast history of reggaetón](https://time.com/6104431/loud-spotify-podcast-reggaeton-history/) — Journalistic synthesis documents overlooked Panamanian reggae en español and Jamaican dancehall contributions alongside Puerto Rican 1990s formalization.
- [AllMusic reggaeton overview](https://www.allmusic.com/style/reggaeton-ma0000002976) — Specialist overview used for category cross-reference, not a universal point of origin.

## Assessment

Kept c.1990 as a coarse marker for the 1990s emergence/formalization of reggaeton while distinguishing earlier Panamanian reggae en español and Jamaican dancehall influences from the Puerto Rican underground precursor. Removed arbitrary 2001 golden-age and 2018 established phases. Confidence: medium-high for 1990s emergence; the genre has multiple foundational geographies and changing naming.

## Remaining uncertainty

Sources dispute whether the start should be assigned to Panama’s 1980s/early-1990s Spanish reggae, Puerto Rico’s 1990s underground or their transnational interaction.
