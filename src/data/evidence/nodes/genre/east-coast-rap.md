## Sources checked

- [Bucktown vs. “G” Thang — The Oxford Handbook of Hip Hop Music](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/34741/chapter-abstract/296555612) — Scholarly chapter examines how 1990s press and recorded music constructed East/West identities as an ideological and market distinction.
- [Rap on the Radio: Making Hip Hop into Hit Pop, 1986–1994 — University of Virginia](https://libraetd.lib.virginia.edu/public_view/m039k510c) — History dissertation documents rap’s late-1980s/early-1990s radio mainstreaming; supports broader period context, not a singular East Coast sound.
- [East Coast Rap Music Genre Overview — AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/genre/east-coast-rap-ma0000002563) — Reference overview contrasts regional scenes and notes West Coast dominance in the 1990s; direct page returned 403.
- [The American Historical Review, “To Live and Die in Hip-Hop”](https://academic.oup.com/ahr/article-abstract/127/1/423/6573685) — Review places hip-hop’s roots in 1970s Bronx and later 1990s globalization; helps distinguish hip-hop origin from the later East Coast regional label.

## Assessment

The graph had hip-hop’s early 1970s origin standing in for East Coast rap and treated 1990 and 2010 as popularity phases without evidence. The sources support a 1980s regionalization context and a particularly visible 1990s coast distinction, not a fixed sound or dated golden age.

## Remaining uncertainty

The label’s initial use is not pinned to one year, and “East Coast” covers diverse local scenes. The 1980s decade is a cautious regionalization marker rather than the origin of New York hip-hop.
