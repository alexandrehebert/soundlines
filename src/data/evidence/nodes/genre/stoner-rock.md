## Sources checked

- [Cambridge Companion to Metal Music, “Get Your Double Kicks on Route 666”](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/cambridge-companion-to-metal-music/get-your-double-kicks-on-route-666/9B2F59F9AEC8DE60D2D7E13FD68FF3D2) — Scholarly history contextualizes the genre’s 1990s US emergence and links to heavy metal, desert rock and scene formation.
- [Cambridge, Popular Music Genres: Heavy Metal](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/popular-music-genres/heavy-metal-noise-for-the-boys/5F31F375F07C9524230FA73820AA13E6) — Genre scholarship cautions that heavy-metal boundaries and derivative scenes are contested.
- [AllMusic stoner metal overview](https://www.allmusic.com/style/stoner-metal-ma0000011961) — Specialist genre reference cross-checks sound and lineage but does not substantiate a 2000 end.
- [Metal Hammer, guide to stoner metal](https://www.loudersound.com/music/albums/beginners-guide-to-stoner-metal) — Specialist scene overview traces 1990s takeoff through Kyuss and later worldwide festivals.

## Assessment

Moved emergence from c.1988 to c.1990 for the better-documented Kyuss/Sleep 1990s scene; removed 2000 endpoint and phases implying a brief popularity cycle. The style draws on earlier hard rock/metal and remains active. Confidence: medium-high for 1990s codification, lower for exact stylistic origin.

## Remaining uncertainty

The source taxonomy overlaps stoner metal, desert rock and psychedelic rock; the graph currently treats them together.
