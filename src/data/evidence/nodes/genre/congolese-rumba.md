## Sources checked

- [Jesse Samba Wheeler, “Rumba Lingala as Colonial Resistance,” Image & Narrative](https://www.imageandnarrative.be/inarchive/worldmusica/jessesambawheeler.htm) — Scholarly article dates Rumba Lingala formation to late 1940s–1950s, notes urban emergence in the 1940s, and uses 1948 as its first-available-recordings baseline.
- [Bob W. White, “Congolese Rumba and Cosmopolitanisms,” Cahiers d’études africaines](https://journals.openedition.org/etudesafricaines/pdf/161) — Journal article documents the arrival of local commercial recording in the 1940s and the 1948 Olympia catalog of more than 200 African titles, contextualizing the recording infrastructure rather than a single genre birth date.
- [Belgian FPS Foreign Affairs, “The Congolese rumba: symbol of unity, joy and cultural expression”](https://diplomatie.belgium.be/en/policy/policy-areas/highlighted/congolese-rumba-symbol-unity-joy-and-cultural-expression) — Official cultural overview dates the genre’s origins to 1950s–60s Leopoldville and documents orchestral popularity and cross-border spread; this contrasts with scholarship tracing an earlier 1940s emergence.
- [University of Birmingham research record, Katrien Pype, “Remediations of Congolese urban dance music in Kinshasa”](https://research.birmingham.ac.uk/en/publications/remediations-of-congolese-urban-dance-music-in-kinshasa/) — Peer-reviewed article describes the continuing popularity of Congolese rumba in the 1950s–70s and subsequent reuse of older songs in later media.
- [UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage, “Congolese rumba”](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/congolese-rumba-01711?trk=article-ssr-frontend-pulse_little-text-block) — Official record indexes inscription in 2021; full page is blocked by a human-verification interstitial, so used only for the indexed inscription date.
- [Ohio State Origins, “The Year of Africa, 1960: Rumba and Pan-Africanism”](https://origins.osu.edu/article/year-africa-1960-rumba-pan-africanism-Kariba?language_content_entity=en) — Existing citation attempted; page inaccessible in this pass and not relied upon.

## Assessment

The 1940s start is supported by music scholarship, but the Belgian FPS overview dates Congolese rumba’s origins to the 1950s–60s. The sources appear to use different boundaries: Wheeler discusses the earlier urban Rumba Lingala formation, while the official overview foregrounds the orchestral style that became popular in Leopoldville. Keep the broad 1940s emergence with medium confidence and make the distinction explicit. Retain the 1950s–60s consolidation marker at medium confidence, changing its visual label from “golden-age” to “established” because the sources document expansion and popularity, not a measurable peak. Remove unsupported 1970/2000 phases and the inaccessible Ohio State link; open-ended span is appropriate.

## Remaining uncertainty

The exact boundary between early Rumba Lingala and the later Congolese rumba style varies by source; 1948 is an earliest-recorded baseline, not a definitive invention date.
