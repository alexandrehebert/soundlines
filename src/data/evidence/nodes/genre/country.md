## Assessment

Keep the 1920s period start as a decade-level marker for commercial country recording, not for the much older rural traditions from which the music drew. The museum resource gives a useful chronology: an early recording in 1922, commercial release in 1923, then the larger 1927 Bristol Sessions. Remove the 1935 “golden-age” and 2010 “established” phases; neither date marks a sourced genre-wide transition. The broad tradition has no defensible end date. No graph-level confidence field exists; the phase marker is rated medium because “country” predates its commercial recording category.

## Remaining uncertainty

Country is a broad, evolving category with contested roots and regional forms; the 1920s date anchors the recording industry’s category, not the music’s full cultural history.
