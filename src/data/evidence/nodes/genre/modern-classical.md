## Sources checked

- [Oxford Academic: Music since the First World War](https://academic.oup.com/book/49273?searchresult=1) — Oxford music history surveys changing modern-composer practices through much of the 20th century rather than positing one unified style.
- [University of Hamburg dissertation on contemporary classical music terminology](https://ediss.sub.uni-hamburg.de/bitstream/ediss/8963/1/FINAL%20Version%20Thesis%20CCM_HfMT0421_ErsatzLebenslauf.pdf) — Research distinguishes contemporary music from “modern music,” modernist music and modern classical music, documenting unstable terminology.
- [University of Nebraska–Lincoln: Modernist Music](https://english.unl.edu/sbehrendt/courses/4802Modernism/ModernistMusicSp2017.html) — University course resource places musical modernism’s beginnings in the 1890s while distinguishing it from the wider modern-classical umbrella.

## Assessment

Retained the approximate 1890 onset only as the late-19th-century modernist edge of this broad umbrella. Removed the 1950 “established” phase, which falsely implies a single lineage or prominence curve. Confidence is medium because the term’s scope varies across institutions and audiences.

## Remaining uncertainty

“Modern classical” is a loose public-facing label; users may mean modernist music, post-1970 contemporary classical music, or a broad classical tradition.
