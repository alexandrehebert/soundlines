## Sources checked

- [University of Michigan Press, What Is Post-Punk?](https://www.fulcrum.org/concern/monographs/pr76f523b?locale=en&per_page=10&view=gallery) — Monograph studies the late-1970s/early-1980s UK and US music press and explicitly dates its focus 1977–82; shows label and boundaries are historically constructed.
- [Oxford Handbook of Punk Rock](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/38573) — Scholarly handbook places punk’s high years around 1976–84 and treats post-punk’s ongoing history and international spread.
- [Cambridge Companion to Krautrock, “Krautrock and British Post-Punk”](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/cambridge-companion-to-krautrock/krautrock-and-british-postpunk/F3E4EE8B0100BE9799D9BC9EC6DA5B8E) — Scholarly bibliography cites Reynolds’s established periodization Rip It Up and Start Again: Postpunk 1978–1984.

## Assessment

The 1977–c.1984 interval is a conventional UK/US historical movement frame, supported by scholarly histories; it should not imply the sound or label stopped afterward. Removed the lone golden-age prominence marker because it duplicated the full period and was not measured. Confidence: medium-high for this commonly used frame, lower for global universality.

## Remaining uncertainty

Regional post-punk scenes and later uses of the term extend beyond this concentrated first-wave period.
