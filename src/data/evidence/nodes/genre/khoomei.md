# Tuvan throat singing (xöömei)

Graph ID `genre:khoomei`

**Review status** · Unsupported phases removed; recording chronology corroborated

## Sources checked

- [Morten Abildsnes, The New Research of Tuva (2017), DOI 10.25178/nit.2017.2.6](https://nit.tuva.asia/nit/en/article/view/706) — Article abstract and references read. Discography starts with seven Tuvan music discs released in Moscow in 1934, followed by seventeen in 1958. Supports a recording-history anchor, not an origin date. Full Russian article not reviewed.
- [Smithsonian National Museum of Asian Art](https://asia-archive.si.edu/podcast/throat-singers-from-tuva-huun-huur-tu/) — Concert documentation and explanatory notes read. Records the ensemble’s 2002 performance, explains harmonic techniques and pastoral context. Does not establish an exact beginning for the tradition.
- [Smithsonian Folkways](https://folkways.si.edu/throat-singing-unique-vocalization-three-cultures/world/music/article/smithsonian) — Page opened and indexed Tuva section checked: describes transmission, changing gender participation and professional performance. No support for a 1915 peak.
- [UNESCO: Mongolian art of singing, Khoomei](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/mongolian-art-of-singing-khoomei-00210?RL=00210) — Direct access returned human verification; search-index excerpt describes Mongolian communities across several countries, including Tuva. Contextual comparison, not evidence that Tuvan practice began or entered a new phase in 2010.

## Decision

Remove the unsupported 1900 emergence, 1915 golden age and 2010 transition. Use 1934 as a medium-confidence documentation anchor for an already established oral tradition, explicitly not its origin. One continuing phase avoids inventing a popularity curve. Further research could move the documentation boundary earlier; precise origins remain unresolved.
