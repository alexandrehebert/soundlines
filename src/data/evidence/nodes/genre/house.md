## Sources checked

- [Oxford University Press: Do You Remember House?, “The Warehouse and The Music Box”](https://academic.oup.com/book/5894/chapter-abstract/149189393) — Scholarly chapter grounds house emergence in queer Black/Latino sociality and the Warehouse/Music Box, drawing on oral histories and recordings.
- [Oxford University Press: Do You Remember House?, Introduction](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9780190698430_A35646664/preview-9780190698430_A35646664.pdf) — Book introduction situates Chicago house flourishing from 1977 to 1992 and stresses queer-of-color club networks.
- [Official Charts: “Jack Your Body”](https://www.officialcharts.com/songs/steve-silk-hurley-jack-your-body/) — UK chart archive dates first chart appearance to January 10, 1987; this source documents one market’s crossover.
- [Georgia Public Broadcasting: From the Warehouse to the world](https://www.gpb.org/news/2023/03/02/the-warehouse-the-world-chicago-and-the-birth-of-house-music) — Oral-history-based public media feature discusses Chicago scene and the commonly cited 1984 “On & On” record milestone.

## Assessment

The circa-1980 start in the graph captured the club era but was less precise than the scholarly source’s account of the Warehouse and Music Box scene beginning from 1977. This does not mean the completed genre or first house record existed that year. Early records followed in the 1980s, with 1987 UK chart crossover; these milestones should not be collapsed into one global “golden age.” The open period remains appropriate for this continuing, internally diverse genre family.

## Remaining uncertainty

The Warehouse is foundational but not the only origin story; first-record claims and the genre’s naming are debated. The 1977 start is an anchor for club-scene formation, not a definitive invention date.
