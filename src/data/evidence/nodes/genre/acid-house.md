## Sources checked

- [Roland — DJ Pierre and Phuture interview](https://articles.roland.com/lifetime-achievement-dj-pierre-and-phuture/) — Interview-led account says Ron Hardy received a cassette in 1985; “Acid Tracks” was officially pressed by Trax in 1987. Helpful for separating circulation, club adoption and release dates.
- [DJ Mag — Phuture, “Acid Tracks”](https://djmag.com/content/game-changers-phuture-acid-tracks) — Interview with DJ Pierre corroborates TB-303 experimentation, early Chicago club circulation and the 1987 release; underscores Phuture’s pivotal role without erasing the surrounding Chicago scene.
- [British Culture Archive — Acid House: The People’s Archive](https://britishculturearchive.co.uk/acid-house/) — Photo archive account traces Chicago origins and cross-Atlantic pathways; distinguishes Shoom and Haçienda histories and documents spread across Britain by summer 1988.
- [Jones & Walker Churchman, “It was like a big union” (Contemporary British History)](https://ueaeprints.uea.ac.uk/id/eprint/97031/) — Peer-reviewed history analyzes competing popular memories and media framings of UK acid house/rave from late 1980s to late 1990s; useful caution against treating the UK scene as one simple story.
- [Oxford Modern British History article (legacy citation)](https://academic.oup.com/tcbh/article/34/2/299/7040380) — Publisher page redirected to inaccessible content service; relevant abstract/indexed record was visible, but full article not directly checked, so removed from graph sources.
- [British Journal of Sociology, “Acid House and Thatcherism”](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1080/00071310120109348) — Search abstract confirms focus on the late-1980s UK subculture; page fetch blocked, so not used as a graph source.

## Assessment

Kept the 1985 emergence marker but sharpened it: the year refers to an early cassette reaching Ron Hardy, while the official pressing was in 1987. Retained 1988 as the documented British expansion marker, but labeled it geographically. Improved confidence to high for the overall Chicago-to-UK account based on independent music-history reporting, a primary participant interview, archival evidence and peer-reviewed history. Removed Wikipedia and the unverified Oxford citation.

## Remaining uncertainty

Acid house can mean the Chicago sound or Britain’s broader late-1980s club/rave movement. The genre’s early-release credit and exact boundaries are contested; phases intentionally separate the two contexts.
