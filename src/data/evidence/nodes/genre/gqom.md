## Sources checked

- [Cambridge journal Africa: gqom and Afrikaans music](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/africa/article/new-currents-old-boundaries-exploring-the-relationship-between-streaming-platforms-and-afrikaans-music/836CA8C06ECDC0789D07E33BDB738609) — Peer-reviewed article cites gqom emergence around 2008 in Durban and contextualizes later regional migration.
- [Journal of the Musical Arts in Africa: Historicising gqom](https://journals.co.za/doi/abs/10.2989/18121004.2021.2020964) — Peer-reviewed study positions gqom in relation to kwaito and its distinct production/self-definition.
- [Resident Advisor: A History of Gqom in Ten Tracks](https://ra.co/features/3917) — Scene history says DIY gqom spread through WhatsApp in early 2010s and became predominant in Durban underground.
- [DJ Mag: DJ Lag profile](https://djmag.com/longreads/dj-lag-gqom-king-conquers-globe) — Long-form profile describes gqom’s late-2000s/early-2010s origins and Lag’s 2016 international breakthrough.

## Assessment

The graph’s 2010 start was too late for scholarship placing emergence around 2008. Sources separate local formation from early-2010s circulation and later international exposure. The previous “golden age” beginning 2015 was unsupported and is removed; 2016 serves only as a documented global-visibility marker.

## Remaining uncertainty

Gqom chronology is partly oral and network-based; dates may vary depending on whether one means early Durban productions, a named genre, or wider circulation.
