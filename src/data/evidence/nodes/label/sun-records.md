## Sources checked

- [Sun Records — History](https://sunrecords.com/history/) — Official history dates Phillips’s launch of the record company to February 1952.
- [National Park Service — Sun Record Company / Memphis Recording Service](https://npgallery.nps.gov/NRHP/GetAsset/a456618c-7d74-4764-8d73-42e46dfa35cc) — National Register documentation identifies the first Sun label release as Johnny London’s “Drivin’ Slow,” March 27, 1952.
- [National Endowment for the Humanities — Sam Phillips’s Sun Records](https://www.neh.gov/humanities/2016/januaryfebruary/statement/the-birth-rock-%E2%80%98n%E2%80%99-roll-found-sam-phillips%E2%80%99s-sun-records) — Historical account places Sun’s opening in Memphis in 1952 and contextualizes its role in early rock and roll.

## Assessment

Retained 1952; sources distinguish Phillips’s earlier Memphis Recording Service from the Sun label itself. Avoid presenting Sun as the sole origin of rock and roll: its importance rests on a particular studio, repertoire, and influential recordings.

## Remaining uncertainty

Exact founding day is given by the official label history; the National Register record corroborates the first label release date.
