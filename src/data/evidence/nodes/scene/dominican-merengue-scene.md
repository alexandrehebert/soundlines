## Sources checked

- [UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage: Dominican merengue](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/merengue-music-and-dance-of-the-dominican-republic-01162) — Identifies the country’s north as the practice’s cradle and Santo Domingo as a recurring festival city.
- [Dominican Ministry of Foreign Affairs: Music](https://mirex.gob.do/cultura/musica/) — Describes Cibao as the source region of merengue dominicano and distinguishes regional forms.
- [Dominican Ministry of Culture: Merengue under Trujillo](https://cultura.gob.do/noticias/conferencia-aborda-la-evolucion-del-merengue-y-su-vinculo-con-la-dictadura-de-trujillo-en-la-feria-del-libro/) — Summarizes how orchestras and bandleaders helped consolidate merengue as national music from the 1930s to 1960s.
- [Latin American Research Review: Bachata’s imagined communities](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/latin-american-research-review/article/from-radio-guarachita-to-el-tieto-eshow-bachatas-imagined-communities/B1507CFD80DC5775668D191F2478AF57) — Scholarly study discusses merengue de orquesta as an urban expression and its privileged place under Trujillo.

## Assessment

A Dominican national dance-music tradition with distinct regional histories. The 1930s period marks state-backed orchestral nationalization, not the genre’s origin; Cibao is central to merengue típico, and Santo Domingo is a major festival and performance center. Confidence is medium because the entry brings together regional forms and a contested origin history.
