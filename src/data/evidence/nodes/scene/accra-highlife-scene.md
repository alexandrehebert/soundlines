## Sources checked

- [UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage: Highlife music and dance](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/highlife-music-and-dance-02141?RL=02141) — Documents highlife as Ghanaian music with changing practice and intergenerational transmission.
- [Cambridge University Press: Dance subcultures in Accra](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/sources-and-methods-in-african-history/rain-or-shine-we-gonna-rock-dance-subcultures-and-identity-construction-in-accra-ghana/02808FD24418BB5F63E278FA2AC22ECB) — Historical chapter documents highlife among the popular forms shaping youth culture in Accra, in dialogue with other local and transnational styles.
- [Journal of African Arts and Culture: Indigenous origins of highlife](https://journals.uew.edu.gh/index.php/jaac/article/view/507) — Peer-reviewed article dates highlife’s emergence across the 1920s–1970s and identifies a 1928 recording; it also discusses indigenous strands and debated origins.

## Assessment

Accra was one of several coastal Ghanaian centers in a transnational genre whose early recorded and dance-band history reaches the 1920s; the better-known independence-era youth scene is a later phase, not the genre’s starting point. Overall confidence: high.
