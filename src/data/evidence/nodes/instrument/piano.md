## Sources checked

- [The Piano: The Pianofortes of Bartolomeo Cristofori — The Metropolitan Museum of Art](https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/the-piano-the-pianofortes-of-bartolomeo-cristofori-1655-1731) — Museum essay cites the 1700 Medici inventory and says piano became a leading instrument for professionals and amateurs in the last quarter of the eighteenth century.
- [Square Piano — Rijksmuseum](https://www.rijksmuseum.nl/en/collection/object/Square-piano--98ccde85f4542829be4e0ff01f1f15cf) — Object record dates a Zumpe & Buntebart square piano to 1773 and places wider popularity around 1785.
- [History of Stringed Keyboard Instruments: The Invention of the Piano — Cambridge University Press](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/history-of-stringed-keyboard-instruments/invention-of-the-piano/EF896FE4B4182F58C6C0375D072AC350) — Scholarly chapter describes Cristofori’s 1700 instrument and the Medici inventory; notes hybrid instruments persisted during later competition with harpsichords.

## Assessment

The graph’s c.1700 start and late-eighteenth-century adoption phase are independently supported by the Met and Rijksmuseum. Kept the adoption phase broad and European; removed both Wikipedia sources. Current wording distinguishes the historical prototype from the modern grand piano.

## Remaining uncertainty

“Piano” evolved across multiple forms and markets; the phase describes European adoption, not a single worldwide transition.
