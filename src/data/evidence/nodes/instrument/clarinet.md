## Sources checked

- [Wikipedia, original citation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clarinet) — Full page retrieved successfully in this review; replaces previous access failure. Used as overview.
- [Musical Instruments Museum, Brussels](https://www.mim.be/en/collection-piece/clarinet) — Record for Jacob Denner clarinet, inv. 0912: caption dates it to first quarter of 18th century. Text challenges traditional father attribution and favors Jacob. Its separate metadata says early 17th century, contradicting caption and discussion; do not adopt that field.
- [Metropolitan Museum of Art](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/506724) — Read record for German two-key clarinet, 1750–70. Attributes invention circa 1700 to J. C. Denner, unlike Brussels. Notes replacement keys and composite mouthpiece/barrel.
- [Gregory Barrett, Northern Illinois University](https://www.niu.edu/gbarrett/resources/development.shtml) — Read development essay: earliest Nuremberg records 1710; surviving early instruments by Jacob; five-key instrument known by 1770; Mozart wrote for low register. Technical history supplies approximate development marker, not popularity data.
- [National Park Service, New Orleans jazz history](https://www.nps.gov/jazz/learn/historyculture/jazz_history.htm) — Read history: turn-of-century front line usually cornet, clarinet and trombone. Supports ensemble role; not exclusive melodic leadership or a global clarinet peak.
- [Philharmonia, concerto recording](https://philharmonia.co.uk/products/phibbs-and-mozart-clarinet-concertos/) — Read orchestra release page: documents Mozart K.622 for basset clarinet and continued concerto performance. Does not establish 1780 as a golden-age threshold.
- [Philharmonia Baroque, October 2024 program](https://philharmonia.org/wp-content/uploads/2024-Oct-PBO-Program-Book-Web.pdf) — Search excerpt located Mozart discussion; full PDF returned 403. No retained date depends on this unexamined text.

## Assessment

Keep circa-1700 precision and medium confidence: competing institutional accounts agree on broad period, not inventor identity. Name the Denner family without deciding father versus son. Replace unsupported peak chronology with explicitly editorial development anchors. Qualify jazz leadership and replace subjective Mozart-favourite language with documented repertoire. Links to genres/artists are deferred.

## Remaining uncertainty

Exact inventor and year remain disputed. No quantitative worldwide prominence series supports a golden age; the 1770 marker reflects instrument development only.
