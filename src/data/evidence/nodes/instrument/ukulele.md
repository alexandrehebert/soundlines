## Sources checked

- [Ukulele — The Metropolitan Museum of Art](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/501343) — Museum object is dated 1880 and explains the 1879 arrival of Madeiran braguinha players and local manufacture by Nunes, Dias and Espírito Santo by 1888.
- [New Additions Enhance Musical Stories in the Oceania Gallery — Musical Instrument Museum](https://mim.org/news/new-additions-enhance-musical-stories-in-the-oceania-gallery/) — Museum account describes early-1880s Hawaiian development from Madeiran instruments and names the three makers.
- [School’s Out! — Library of Congress, Now See Hear!](https://blogs.loc.gov/now-see-hear/2014/06/schools-out/) — Library history distinguishes the 1879 introduction from later Hawaiian embrace and 1920s mainland popularity.
- [Hawaiian Song — Library of Congress](https://www.loc.gov/collections/songs-of-america/articles-and-essays/musical-styles/american-indian-and-native-alaskan/hawaiian/) — LOC essay situates ukulele among late-nineteenth-century Hawaiian instrument innovations and early-recording traditions.

## Assessment

The c.1880 start is supported as a local development date and should not be conflated with the arrival of Madeiran migrants in 1879. The 1888 phase is a locally specific maker milestone. The evidence also shows an independent 1920s mainland popularity wave, so the timeline avoids treating it as origin or worldwide establishment.

## Remaining uncertainty

The instrument emerged through adaptation by Hawaiian and Portuguese makers; assigning invention to a single person or exact day would overstate the record.
