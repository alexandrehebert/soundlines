## Sources checked

- [Library of Congress, original citation](https://www.loc.gov/collections/songs-of-america/articles-and-essays/musical-styles/american-indian-and-native-alaskan/hawaiian/) — Direct retrieval returned 403; not counted as full-text verification.
- [Guardian, original citation](https://www.theguardian.com/music/2010/aug/17/ukulele) — Retrieved; supports four strings and vaudeville/pop use. Origin shorthand is superseded by museum accounts.
- [Metropolitan Museum of Art](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/501343) — Collection object dated circa 1880. Text distinguishes Madeiran arrivals in 1879, local adaptation and production by Nunes, Dias and Espirito Santo by 1888. Supports Hawaiian development rather than simple importation of the finished instrument.
- [Musical Instrument Museum, Phoenix](https://mim.org/news/new-additions-enhance-musical-stories-in-the-oceania-gallery/) — May 2026 exhibit article read. Dates Hawaiian development to early 1880s from machete and rajao; describes a surviving 1890s Espirito Santo instrument and later local makers.
- [Smithsonian National Museum of American History](https://americanhistory.si.edu/explore/stories/5-things-you-probably-didnt-know-about-ukulele) — Two direct retrievals returned server errors; search excerpt not counted as reviewed full text.
- [Library of Congress, alternate object record](https://www.loc.gov/item/2021669826/) — Alternate collection item also returned 403.

## Assessment

The existing circa-1880 date holds up. Increase confidence to medium using two museum accounts while keeping precision approximate. Broaden the predecessor claim beyond machete alone and preserve local Hawaiian adaptation. Separate emergence from documented local manufacture; do not convert arrival year into exact invention year.

## Remaining uncertainty

Original Library of Congress citation remains inaccessible, as does the Smithsonian corroboration. The 1888 phase reflects local production rather than quantified popularity. Relationships deferred.
