## Sources checked

- [Demian’s Accordion Patent — The Classical Free-Reed](https://www.henrydoktorski.com/free-reed/history/demian.html) — Reproduces an English summary and images of the 1829 Vienna patent; page notes the translation is not literal.
- [Accordion making — Institut pour les Savoir-Faire Français](https://www.institut-savoirfaire.fr/savoir-faire-rare/facture-daccordeon) — French craft institution recounts Demian’s patent, later design changes and European popularization; gives regional chronology rather than a global peak.
- [“Alexandre Père & Fils” accordion — The Metropolitan Museum of Art](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/503502) — Museum object circa 1831 shows rapid post-patent development of a second button row and a more elaborate Paris design.
- [Cumbia Podcast — Indiana University Libraries](https://libraries.indiana.edu/cumbia) — University archives resource contextualizes regional Colombian cumbia; used only to support accordion’s presence within diverse cumbia traditions.

## Assessment

The 1829 date is specifically the patent and naming of Demian’s chord instrument; it should not imply free reeds or the modern accordion began then. The previous 1830 “established” phase was too early and unspecific; it is moved to the documented European spread around 1860. Removed all unsupported 1900/1950 global popularity phases.

## Remaining uncertainty

The early accordion was one design among a wider free-reed history; later instruments and regional adoption have separate chronologies.
