## Sources checked

- [Leedy Manufacturing Company — Indiana Historical Bureau](https://www.in.gov/history/state-historical-markers/find-a-marker/find-historical-markers-by-county/indiana-historical-markers-by-county/leedy-manufacturing-company/) — State historical account cites Leedy catalogs and patent records, describing Winterhoff’s experiments, a 1919 patent and the instrument’s familiarity in dance bands by 1927.
- [US Patent 1,304,435, Musical Instrument — United States Patent Office](https://patents.google.com/patent/US1304435A/en) — Primary patent filed in 1916 and issued 1919 to Herman Winterhoff/Leedy; its generic title cautions against treating it as the complete modern vibraphone.
- [Extended Performance Techniques and Compositional Style in Solo Concert Vibraphone Music — University of North Texas dissertation](https://digital.library.unt.edu/ark:/67531/metadc9030/m2/1/high_res_d/dissertation.pdf) — Historical dissertation dates the refined rotating-disc vibrato mechanism to 1921 and explains naming by Leedy.
- [Lionel Hampton — National Endowment for the Arts](https://www.arts.gov/honors/jazz/lionel-hampton) — NEA dates Hampton’s uptake to 1929 and his recorded debut to 1930, establishing an early jazz landmark.
- [Vibraphone — Grinnell College Musical Instrument Collection](https://omeka-s.grinnell.edu/s/MusicalInstruments/item/2395) — College collection summary dates a marketed Leedy design to 1921 and notes its rapid adoption by dance-band and jazz players.

## Assessment

The 1921 start understated earlier Leedy experimentation and blurred prototype, patent, refined design and naming. Patent and historical-bureau evidence place experiments by 1916; music-history and collection sources support the mature rotating-disc instrument around 1921. The 1930 jazz phase is supported by NEA, but no overall “golden age” is inferred. Removed Wikipedia.

## Remaining uncertainty

Credit and design lineage are contested between Leedy/Winterhoff and Deagan/Scluter; source descriptions use different terms for early steel marimbas, vibraphones and vibraharps.
