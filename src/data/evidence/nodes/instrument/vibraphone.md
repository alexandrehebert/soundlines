## Sources checked

- [Indiana Historical Bureau: Leedy Manufacturing Company](https://www.in.gov/history/state-historical-markers/find-a-marker/find-historical-markers-by-county/indiana-historical-markers-by-county/leedy-manufacturing-company/) — Uses Leedy catalogs, patents, and contemporary press: Winterhoff’s 1916 patent application/1919 grant predates the instrument’s 1921 refinement; a 1928 catalog documents motorized resonator fans and period use in dance groups.
- [University of North Texas dissertation: A Brief History of the Vibraphone](https://digital.library.unt.edu/ark:/67531/metadc9030/m2/1/high_res_d/dissertation.pdf) — Describes bars, resonators, motor-operated rotating discs, the 1921 naming/refinement, vaudeville recordings around 1924, and subsequent jazz adoption; chronology cites earlier percussion histories.
- [National Endowment for the Arts: Lionel Hampton](https://www.arts.gov/honors/jazz/lionel-hampton) — Dates Hampton’s vibraphone use with the Hite band to 1929 and his recorded debut to 1930; confirms his bands’ popularity in the 1940s–50s, but that is artist-level evidence, not proof of instrument-wide peak prevalence.
- [Vibraphone (Wikipedia, original graph citation)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vibraphone) — Checked the 1921 instrument history and jazz-adoption summary against Indiana historical sources, UNT dissertation, and NEA biography.
- [Grinnell College Musical Instrument Collection: Vibraphone](https://omeka-s.grinnell.edu/s/MusicalInstruments/item/2395) — Search extract corroborates 1921 invention and early dance-band/jazz use; page itself returned HTTP 403, so not treated as independently verified.

## Assessment

The evidence separates Winterhoff’s 1916 patent work from the refined, named vibraphone in 1921. Indiana’s page grounds mechanism and contemporary use in catalogs and press; the UNT dissertation supplies chronology; NEA independently documents jazz adoption by 1929/1930. Replaced low-confidence “golden-age” and 1950 markers with an evidence-based 1930 jazz-establishment marker.

## Remaining uncertainty

Sources disagree slightly on the first commercial recording year (around 1924 in the dissertation versus 1930 as Hampton’s jazz recording debut); these refer to different milestones. Grinnell page could not be opened, and no source here establishes an instrument-wide popularity curve.
