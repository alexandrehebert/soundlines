## Sources checked

- [Claves, Cuba Music](https://www.cubamusic.com/es-ES/Pages/PageDetailCubaMusicalInstruments/Claves) — Describes the instrument as two hardwood sticks and attributes its Havana use in the 16th–17th centuries to Cuban ethnographer Fernando Ortiz; reports later use in rumba, punto cubano, and dance ensembles.
- [National Museum of the American Latino: “Las raíces de la salsa”](https://latino.si.edu/es/exhibitions/puro-ritmo/qr/la-vitrina-las-raices-de-la-salsa) — Smithsonian exhibit identifies the claves as two wooden sticks and describes son/rumba clave patterns as foundational to salsa; direct page returned 500, so relevant text was checked via search index.
- [U.S. National Park Service: Materiales—A Digital Salsa Exhibit](https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/materiales-a-digital-salsa-exhibit.htm) — NPS describes claves as vital to salsa ensemble timing and clave rhythms as musical core.
- [Smithsonian National Museum of American History: Claves used by Mongo Santamaría](https://www.si.edu/object/claves-used-mongo-santamaria%3Anmah_1290068) — Existing graph object record could not be opened (404); not relied on for claims.

## Assessment

The early-date claim is traceable but rests on a secondary account invoking Ortiz; confidence is therefore medium, not high. Smithsonian/NPS sources strongly support current function in salsa and Afro-Cuban ensembles. Removed the graph’s unsubstantiated popularity timeline.

## Remaining uncertainty

The 16th–17th-century Havana use claim needs a direct Ortiz text or specialist history to establish the primary documentation behind the attribution.
