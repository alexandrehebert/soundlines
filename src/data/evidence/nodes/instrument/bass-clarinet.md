## Sources checked

- [Philharmonia instrument directory, original citation](https://philharmonia.co.uk/resources/instruments/) — Directory fetched; links to specific bass clarinet page but does not substantiate an origin year.
- [Philharmonia bass clarinet guide](https://philharmonia.co.uk/resources/instruments/bass-clarinet/) — Text read, video not reviewed. Describes dark and warm timbres, Mahler usage and an early solo in Mercadante’s Emma d’Antiochia (1834). No origin date.
- [Albert R. Rice, Towards a new history of the bass clarinet](https://www.circb.info/?q=node/12567) — Publicly displayed article read: Mayrhofer example dated 1760–1770; earlier prototypes uncertain. Reclassifies Lot’s 1772 basse-tube as basset horn. Documents 1834/1836 opera use and Sax’s 1838 patent. Page warns other site information requires registration.
- [Albert R. Rice, Earliest Bass Clarinet Music](https://www.circb.info/?q=node/44) — Displayed article read: Mayrhofer circa 1765; Grenser surviving instrument dated 1793; Stockholm performance documented in 1794. Identification of the precise instrument used is inferred. Same author as companion article, not independent corroboration.
- [Vienna Symphonic Library instrument guide](https://www.vsl.co.at/academy/woodwinds/bass-clarinet) — Read: orchestral bass role since mid-19th century and Meyerbeer 1836. Its Lot-1772 origin narrative conflicts with Rice’s instrument reassessment and is not adopted.

## Assessment

Move start from circa 1850 to circa 1765, retaining circa precision and medium confidence for the estimated artifact date. Preserve 1850 only as a scoped orchestral-adoption phase. Philharmonia independently corroborates 1834 opera use. Distinguish first surviving instruments, first documented music and later adoption; do not equate these. Do not adopt Lot as inventor or claim Les Huguenots was the first bass clarinet music.

## Remaining uncertainty

Earliest prototypes cannot be precisely dated. Circa 1765 is an artifact-based timeline anchor, not a proven origin; the two Rice articles are one scholarly voice. Graph relationships remain deferred.
