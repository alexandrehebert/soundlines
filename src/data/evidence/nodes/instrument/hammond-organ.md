## Sources checked

- [Hammond Europe history](https://hammond.eu/Info/Story) — Page read: tonewheel development in 1934 and pipe-organ alternative. Manufacturer promotional language is not quantitative popularity evidence.
- [Hammond B-3 history](https://hammondorganco.com/the-hammond-story-2) — Page read: gives B-3 introduction as 1954 and final electromechanical B-3 production in 1975. Describes gospel, jazz and rock use. B-3 launch differs from Wikipedia’s 1955.
- [Smithsonian early Model A record](https://music.si.edu/object-day/hammond-electric-organ) — Indexed collection description read: manufacture 1934, serial number 1, church purchase 1935. Direct opening returned a request-rejection page; not counted as full retrieval.
- [NEA, Jimmy Smith](https://www.arts.gov/honors/jazz/jimmy-smith) — Biography read: Blue Note recordings 1956–63 and international recognition through 1957 Newport; documents substantial jazz adoption beyond manufacturer promotion.
- [Wikipedia, original citation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hammond_organ) — Retrieved overview: 1935 commercial model and 1955 B-3 date. Used for comparison, not to resolve conflicting B-3 launch years.

## Assessment

Retain circa 1935 as commercial rather than invention boundary. Replace golden-age shading with a sourced 1956 jazz-adoption milestone. Keep 1975 as a documented production fact in this evidence record, not a popularity phase. Narrow the description to supported claims and leave genre links for the deferred relationship audit.

## Remaining uncertainty

Direct Smithsonian retrieval remains blocked. B-3 introduction is variously given as 1954 or 1955; the graph does not assert either as an exact model launch. The 1935 Diapason PDF search lead returned 404 and was not read.
