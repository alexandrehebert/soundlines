## Sources checked

- [Hammond Electric Organ — Smithsonian National Museum of American History](https://music.si.edu/object-day/hammond-electric-organ) — Smithsonian object record documents the 1934 patent, 1934 manufacture and 1935 church purchase of the first organ.
- [Jimmy Smith — National Endowment for the Arts](https://www.arts.gov/honors/jazz/jimmy-smith) — NEA describes Smith’s central role in raising the Hammond B-3 to prominence in jazz beginning in the 1950s.
- [The Hammond B-3 — Hammond Organ](https://hammondorganco.com/the-hammond-story-2) — Manufacturer history dates the B-3 model to 1954 and traces use across gospel, pop, jazz, soul and rock.

## Assessment

The inherited start at 1935 omitted Hammond’s 1934 patent and first unit; revised start now distinguishes invention from sale. The B-3 began in 1954, while Smith’s jazz prominence is documented later in the decade. Kept the specific jazz marker and rejected unsupported global 1955/1975 phases.

## Remaining uncertainty

Company histories are useful for model chronology but may overstate reach; jazz prominence should not stand in for adoption across gospel, home, church and rock settings.
