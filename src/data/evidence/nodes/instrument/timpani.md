## Sources checked

- [Philharmonia directory, original citation](https://philharmonia.co.uk/resources/instruments/) — Retrieved earlier in this continuing instrument pass. Directory links the specific guide; no origin chronology in the directory.
- [Philharmonia timpani guide](https://philharmonia.co.uk/resources/instruments/timpani/) — Text read, video not reviewed. Confirms pedal pitch changes and harmonic/rhythmic role. Page contains apparent copied string labels and an absolute 1675 first-scoring claim; neither is adopted uncritically.
- [Museums Victoria, Harriet Beatrice Boothman](https://collections.museumsvictoria.com.au/articles/2789) — Article read. Cites Blades and Montagu for 16th-century German tension screws and later construction; gives orchestral introduction around 1675. Provides the main scoped chronology.
- [Metropolitan Museum of Art, German kettledrum](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/501905) — 18th-century object record read: seven screw tensioners, copper shell and calfskin head; military/ceremonial pairing with trumpets and horseback use. Corroborates construction and context, not an exact invention date.
- [Vienna Symphonic Library guide](https://www.vsl.co.at/academy/percussion/timpani) — History read: earlier small and large kettledrums, early-16th-century screws, 17th-century orchestral adoption and later pedal mechanisms. Its 1675 Lully opera title is suspect; avoid copying it or treating its first-use narrative as definitive.
- [Grinnell College timpani record](https://omeka-s.grinnell.edu/s/MusicalInstruments/item/673) — Direct fetch returned 403; search excerpt alone not counted as reviewed content.
- [New Evidence for the Origins of Kettledrums](https://www.ibew.org.uk/dvarch/DV06053.pdf) — Search located paper, but PDF retrieval timed out; no historical finding inferred from the unexamined paper.

## Assessment

Replace the unexplained 1700 start with a clearly scoped construction milestone and separate orchestral adoption. Medium confidence applies to century-level development and approximate adoption, not exact years. Preserve tunability and orchestral function while including earlier military context. Modern foot pedals must not be projected back onto early screw-tuned drums.

## Remaining uncertainty

Worldwide kettledrum origins and the 1457 Western European documentation story require further primary-source work; the inaccessible historical paper remains a follow-up. These are not presented as established origin dates. Relationships deferred.
