## Sources checked

- [National Park Service: The Banjo](https://home.nps.gov/blri/learn/historyculture/the-banjo.htm) — NPS traces roots to West African akonting/Jola traditions; describes enslaved Africans’ gourd lutes, early banjos in the American South and Caribbean, 19th-century minstrel-era spread/commercialization, and mid-20th-century folk revival.
- [Library of Congress: African-American Banjo Music research guide](https://guides.loc.gov/African-american-banjo/) — LOC describes African-American banjo lineage as reimagining ancestral instruments with New World materials and connects it to blues, gospel, old-time, bluegrass, and country traditions.
- [Smithsonian: Banjos](https://www.si.edu/spotlight/banjos-smithsonian) — Search excerpt identifies enslaved Africans and descendants in the Caribbean and colonial North America as creators, and references 17th-century records; page returned 404 on direct open, so excerpt is not treated as full verification.
- [Library of Congress catalog: The banjo: America’s African instrument](https://www.loc.gov/item/hlas-bi2020002633/) — Search excerpt supports 17th-century origins and gourd/skin construction among enslaved Africans; direct page returned 403.
- [Banjo (Wikipedia, original graph citation)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banjo) — General account cross-checked against NPS and LOC lineage sources; exact 1687 date was not verified in accessible sources and was broadened to the 17th century.

## Assessment

NPS and LOC support African-American development from West African antecedents and later musical lineages. NPS supports the broad 19th-century and mid-20th-century phase anchors. Downgraded the earliest phase confidence and broadened the exact 1687 year to the 17th century; 19th-century “golden age” remains a qualitative marker for mainstream spread, not an endorsement of minstrelsy.

## Remaining uncertainty

The earliest exact year and geographic sequence (Caribbean versus mainland North America) need accessible primary or specialist scholarship; Smithsonian and LOC catalog pages could not be directly opened.
