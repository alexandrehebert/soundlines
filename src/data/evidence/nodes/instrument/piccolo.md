## Sources checked

- [Philharmonia directory, original citation](https://philharmonia.co.uk/resources/instruments/) — Retrieved during this continuing instrument pass; directory supplies the instrument-specific link, not historical dates.
- [Philharmonia piccolo guide](https://philharmonia.co.uk/resources/instruments/piccolo/) — Text read; video not reviewed. Confirms flute doubling, bright orchestral role, Beethoven Fifth usage and military popularity. Its first-major-work wording is not used as an absolute first-use claim.
- [Vienna Symphonic Library](https://www.vsl.co.at/academy/woodwinds/piccolo) — Read history and notation: early-18th-century keyed forms; early flautino labels can mean recorders; later symphonic integration; C piccolo sounds an octave higher than written. Supports only approximate early dating.
- [Grinnell College instrument collection](https://omeka-s.grinnell.edu/s/MusicalInstruments/item/1252) — Read construction/use record: octave above flute, orchestra inclusion around 1800. Quotes Galpin (1937) attributing introduction to Tromlitz in 1791; conflicts with earlier-development account and is not treated as a secure invention claim.
- [Duke University instrument collection](https://sites.duke.edu/dumic/instruments/woodwinds/north-america/piccolo/) — Read Cloos piccolo record and historical summary: orchestral use in 18th century. General history draws on Britannica; not independent primary evidence of an invention year.
- [Tansella et al., Journal of Imaging (2022)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9605226/) — Article retrieved; abstract and historical-instrument methods examined. Studies an anonymous three-part piccolo dated by comparisons to late-18th/early-19th-century instruments. CT work does not directly determine manufacture year or earliest invention.

## Assessment

Retain the accurate pitch claim, qualified for the normally used instrument, and add documented band use. Distinguish early keyed forms from circa-1800 symphonic adoption. Low confidence on the early numerical marker reflects broad dating and naming ambiguity. The conservation study confirms historic construction evidence but cannot adjudicate first-invention claims.

## Remaining uncertainty

Early chronology remains partly unresolved: identify dated transverse examples or original scores before raising confidence. The circa-1700 start is a broad early-century editorial anchor, not a verified year. Relationships remain deferred.
