## Sources checked

- [Oxford Bibliographies, Troubadours and Trouvères](https://academic.oup.com/reference/62400/reference-article-abstract/555406592) — Dates Occitan troubadour song culture to the twelfth and thirteenth centuries and summarizes manuscript evidence.
- [Oxford Bibliographies in Medieval Studies, Music of the Troubadours and Trouvères](https://academic.oup.com/reference/62384/reference-article-abstract/555057629) — Notes the small number of troubadour melodies preserved with notation and the role of manuscript evidence and scholarly inference.
- [Cambridge University Press, Eight Centuries of Troubadours and Trouvères: Manuscript Sources](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/eight-centuries-of-troubadours-and-trouveres/manuscript-sources/8819FE176E3C0417A42CA532AA9B15D6) — Discusses surviving manuscript sources and transmission of this oral-performance repertory.

## Assessment

The circa 1100–1300 range works as a broad cultural period. Written manuscripts preserve only a portion of performed repertory, often copied later; keep the graph’s caveat that manuscript survival is not the full performance culture.
