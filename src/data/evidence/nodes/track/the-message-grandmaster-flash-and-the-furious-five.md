## Sources checked

- [National Museum of African American History and Culture — The Message](https://nmaahc.si.edu/object/nmaahc_2013.1.3ab) — Museum catalog identifies the 1982 Sugar Hill album/record and group credit.
- [Library of Congress — National Recording Registry essay](https://memory.loc.gov/static/programs/national-recording-preservation-board/documents/TheMessage.pdf) — Registry essay documents the song's social-realist impact and credits the group's shift in rap subject matter.
- [Fresh Air Archive — Melle Mel stands by The Message](https://freshairarchive.org/segments/melle-mel-stands-message) — Interview describes Melle Mel as original vocalist and Duke Bootee's role in bringing the song to him.
- [Wayne Marshall — Hip-Hop’s Irrepressible Refashionability](https://wayneandwax.com/academic/marshall-hiphop-irrepressible.pdf) — Academic account notes the song was principally Duke Bootee's conception, with Melle Mel's vocals, despite the group billing.

## Assessment

Artist/date are confirmed, but the standard group credit obscures authorship and performance roles. Retained Old School Rap at high confidence; the song is a landmark, although Grandmaster Flash did not create or perform the vocal centerpiece.

## Remaining uncertainty

Record catalog credits the group; fuller session credits include Melle Mel and Duke Bootee, while Flash's exact contribution varies across accounts.
