## Sources checked

- [Jeff Wragg, “Just Don’t Call it Trip Hop,” *Organised Sound* (Cambridge University Press)](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/organised-sound/article/abs/just-dont-call-it-trip-hop-reconciling-the-bristol-sound-style-with-the-trip-hop-genre/B4944FEFB7C30977DA7CF0CB3AC07465) — Examines how “Bristol sound” and “trip hop” labels overlap and why some artists reject the label.
- [University of Brighton: “Locating the Bristol sound”](https://research.brighton.ac.uk/en/publications/locating-the-bristol-sound-archiving-music-as-everyday-life/) — Research on how venues and overlapping communities shaped everyday city music.
- [Bristol Museums: music scene collection](https://museums.bristol.gov.uk/narratives.php?irn=7986) — Local collection narrative documents Black Bristol sound-system culture and its links to later styles.

## Assessment

The broad scene description is supported. “Trip hop” is a contested label and only one strand; Bristol’s musical history also includes sound-system, punk, hip-hop, house, rave, and jungle networks. Overall confidence: medium.
