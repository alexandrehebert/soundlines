## Sources checked

- [UNESCO: Safeguarding traditional Swahili music in Zanzibar and the Comoros](https://ich.unesco.org/en/projects/safeguarding-the-songs-of-the-moon-traditional-swahili-music-from-tanzania-unguja-and-pemba-and-the-comoros-00101) — Describes taarab as an essential part of multi-day festivities in Zanzibar and supports local safeguarding work.
- [African Music: Travel on a song—the roots of Zanzibar taarab](https://journal.ru.ac.za/index.php/africanmusic/article/view/1805) — Scholarly journal article describes taarab as a major musical form on Unguja and Pemba.
- [Mwenda Ntarangwi: “Taarab Music in Zanzibar”](https://www.ocf.berkeley.edu/~culturalanalysis/volume2/pdf/volumetwo.pdf) — Academic analysis warns that attributing taarab's origin solely to Zanzibar is misleading, citing older Swahili-coast forms and cross-coastal exchange.

## Assessment

The graph says “associated with the development,” not “originated in Zanzibar”; that distinction matters. Confidence is medium because taarab belongs to a broad Swahili coastal network and origin stories remain debated.
