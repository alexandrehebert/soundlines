## Sources checked

- [Oxford University Press: The Birth of the Nashville Recording Industry](https://academic.oup.com/book/36601/chapter-abstract/321576929) — Music scholar Travis D. Stimeling traces Nashville's recording infrastructure and dates its rise as a significant country-recording center to the mid-1950s.
- [Country Music Hall of Fame and Museum: Historic RCA Studio B](https://www.countrymusichalloffame.org/experiences/studio-b/about-studio-b) — Museum documents Nashville's studio and production networks, including Chet Atkins's management of RCA's Nashville operation.
- [Nashville Public Library: Special Collections and Music Row oral histories](https://library.nashville.gov/blog/2026/04/cousins-not-twins-metro-archives-and-special-collections-explained) — Library describes its Music Row oral-history collections; this supports the city's archival record, not the whole industry claim.

## Assessment

The description correctly characterizes Nashville as a major but non-exclusive center. Country music's history extends well beyond the city, and “central” refers to institutional concentration rather than sole origin. Overall confidence: high.
