## Sources checked

- [Red Bull Music Academy, Mala lecture](https://www.redbullmusicacademy.com/lectures/mala-5-db-to-perfection/) — First-person interview: Mala identifies Coki and himself as Digital Mystikz and dates their first release to 2003; distinguishes DMZ as label and club night.
- [The Arts Desk interview with Coki](https://theartsdesk.com/new-music/theartsdesk-qa-producerdj-coki-digital-mystikz) — Coki independently dates signing a record deal with Mala as Digital Mystikz to 2003.
- [The Guardian oral history of DMZ](https://www.theguardian.com/music/2026/jun/09/the-history-of-dmz-the-label-that-gave-dubstep-its-soul) — Recent oral history distinguishes the DMZ label/party collective (Mala, Coki, Loefah, Sgt Pokes) from Digital Mystikz the duo; dates first DMZ party to March 2005 and describes its scene impact.
- [DJ Mag, Big Apple Records and dubstep](https://djmag.com/features/how-big-apple-records-became-birthplace-dubstep) — Interview-based history corroborates the DMZ label and club-night origin, and explains the broader South London dubstep context.

## Assessment

Corrected the start from 2004 to 2003 based on concordant first-person testimony from Mala and Coki. Replaced Wikipedia with two artist interviews, a recent oral history, and an interview-based scene history. Clarified a common conflation: Digital Mystikz is Mala and Coki; the related DMZ label and party also involved Loefah and MC Sgt Pokes. The 2004/2005 dates more accurately describe DMZ’s label/night milestones, not the duo’s first release. Left phases out because the sources establish important scene milestones but not a well-defined prominence curve for the duo itself.

## Remaining uncertainty

Digital Mystikz naming and early collaboration developed around the first 2003 release; sources do not fix a precise formation date. The graph therefore dates the documented release, not the pair’s earliest collaboration. The duo and the larger DMZ collective should remain distinct.
