## Sources checked

- [National Library of Jamaica, Reggae Exhibition: Desmond Dekker](https://www.nlj.gov.jm/REGGAE%20EXHIBITION/Desmond%20Dekker.htm) — Documents first releases under the Dekker name in 1963, the Aces, major songs, 1960s success, reduced 1970s chart success, and death in 2006. It dates the UK success of “Israelites” to 1968, which conflicts with the UK chart database’s 1969 chart record; likely a release/recording versus chart-year distinction.
- [Official Charts, “The Israelites” by Desmond Dekker and the Aces](https://www.officialcharts.com/songs/desmond-dekker-and-the-aces-the-israelites/) — Primary chart record: first entered the UK chart March 25, 1969, peaked at No. 1, and remained in the chart for 14 weeks.
- [The Guardian obituary](https://www.theguardian.com/culture/2006/may/26/uk.obituaries) — Contemporary obituary identifies Dekker as Jamaican, recounts the 1969 hit and US top-ten success, and documents his continued touring shortly before death in 2006.
- [Oxford Dictionary of National Biography, Desmond Dacres entry](https://academic.oup.com/oxford-dictionary-of-national-biography/reference/63486/reference-article-abstract/579968320) — Search result identifies the biographical entry and 1941–2006 lifespan; full page fetch failed, so it was not used to substantiate the graph.

## Assessment

Replaced an unrelated ska-genre citation with Jamaican national-library documentation, the UK Official Charts record, and a contemporary obituary. Corrected the loose 1960s–2000s decade period to 1963–2006: the first recording period is documented by the National Library of Jamaica, and both it and the obituary give 2006 as the end year. Kept the UK chart milestone at 1969, not 1968; the National Library may be referring to an earlier Jamaican record/release. Added restrained phases with medium confidence because the milestones are clear but prominence boundaries are editorial.

## Remaining uncertainty

The National Library and UK chart database differ on whether “Israelites” topped the UK chart in 1968 or 1969. Official Charts directly documents a 1969 chart entry and peak; recording/release dates vary by territory. The Oxford biography’s full text could not be retrieved and is not relied on.
