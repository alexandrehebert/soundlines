## Sources checked

- [DJ Mag, “Believe the Hype!”](https://djmag.com/content/believe-hype-0) — First-person interview: Hype places jungle’s emergence around 1992–93, discusses its reggae, hip-hop, soul, and rave roots, and identifies his role as one contributor among many.
- [Skiddle interview, “Still Smokin”](https://www.skiddle.com/news/all/DJ-Hype-interview-Still-Smokin/31904/) — Interview-based profile dates Exorcist and The Bee (as The Scientist, with Phivos Sebastiane) to 1990, Ganja Records to 1994–96, and describes the transition from reggae sound systems through hardcore to jungle and drum and bass.
- [Data Transmission, 5 Steps with DJ Hype](https://datatransmission.co/blog/5-steps-dj-hype/) — Profile notes a reggae sound system formed in his early teens and later Fantasy FM pirate-radio work; this distinguishes earlier sound-system roots from the 1989 broadcast career marker.
- [Playaz Recordings, label history](https://shop.playaz.co.uk/pages/about-us) — Label’s own history documents True Playaz and the 2006 re-launch as Real Playaz/Playaz Recordings with Hype, Pascal, and Zinc; attributes the label catalogue collectively.
- [DJ Mag, DJ Hype 2008 profile](https://djmag.com/top100djs/2008/71/DJ-Hype) — Contemporary profile documents ongoing international DJ work, Playaz, and a long-running Kiss FM programme by 2008.

## Assessment

Replaced the sole Wikipedia citation with DJ Hype interviews, DJ Mag’s 2013 first-person scene history, the label’s own chronology, and a contemporary DJ profile. Retained 1989 as the start of his pirate-radio/public DJ career; earlier reggae sound-system work is noted but is not treated as a precise professional start. Corrected the distinction between The Scientist collaboration with Phivos Sebastiane (1990) and Hype’s later jungle work. The phases use medium confidence because the historical milestones are clear but their prominence labels are editorial.

## Remaining uncertainty

The sources differ in what they call the start of a career: teenage sound-system work, Fantasy FM broadcasting in 1989, and production releases in 1990. The graph uses the first dated broadcast milestone. Jungle’s emergence was collective; Hype explicitly credits multiple crews and producers. Ganja/Playaz credits should not be conflated with every track released by those labels.
