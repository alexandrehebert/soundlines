## Sources checked

- [Oxford Bibliographies in Music, Hildegard of Bingen](https://academic.oup.com/reference/62400/reference-article-abstract/580096066) — Musicologist Honey Meconi surveys the surviving plainchant and Ordo Virtutum and notes scholarly questions around attribution and dating.
- [International Society of Hildegard von Bingen Studies, Music](https://www.hildegard-society.org/p/music.html) — Specialist scholarly society says most works are generally dated c.1140–1160 but cautions that individual works cannot all be dated accurately.
- [McGill eScholarship, “Hildegard’s Antiphons as a Genre”](https://escholarship.mcgill.ca/downloads/79408022p) — Musicological study cites a contemporary 1148 letter praising her “novel songs,” confirming activity by that point.
- [University of Melbourne, “The Symphonia of Hildegard of Bingen”](https://bpb-ap-se2.wpmucdn.com/blogs.unimelb.edu.au/dist/6/184/files/2017/02/7_Jeffreys-11cqemi.pdf) — Study argues composition began in the 1140s and cites evidence as early as 1141, but notes manuscript and dating ambiguities.
- [Stanford, Hildegard of Bingen: On Music](https://bartholomew.stanford.edu/onmusichildegard/introtext.html) — University teaching resource records a 1178/79 letter defending her community’s singing of her music.

## Assessment

Corrected the previous change after further checking: c.1140–1160 describes the estimated date of the majority of works, not her whole composing career. Contemporary recognition by 1148 supports a start by c.1141, while evidence of her defending performance in 1178/79 supports activity near the end of her life. Set c.1141–1179 as an approximate documented creative career; the graph’s prior 1150 start was too late. Confidence: medium.

## Remaining uncertainty

This is a broad activity interval, not a claim that every piece dates to those endpoints; scholars emphasize the difficulty of dating individual compositions.
