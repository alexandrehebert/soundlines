## Sources checked

- [Divine Official discography](https://divineofficial.com/discography) — Estate/official artist discography dates “Born to Be Cheap” to 1981, the first listed single, and the first album and “Native Love” to 1982.
- [Red Bull Music Academy Daily, Wax Trax! history](https://daily.redbullmusicacademy.com/2014/03/wax-trax-feature/) — Label history independently places Divine’s “Born to Be Cheap” after Wax Trax!’s 1981 release milestone.
- [Official Charts, “You Think You’re a Man”](https://www.officialcharts.com/songs/divine-you-think-youre-a-man/) — Primary UK chart data: entered July 14, 1984 and peaked at No. 16.
- [Stock Aitken Waterman official timeline, 1984](https://www.stockaitkenwaterman.co.uk/1984) — Producer-authored timeline confirms Divine’s “You Think You’re a Man” hit and identifies his distinctive voice and club-scene background.
- [Los Angeles Times obituary](https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1988-03-08-mn-420-story.html) — Contemporary obituary identifies Harris Glenn Milstead as Divine and reports his death in March 1988.
- [AllMusic Divine profile (previous graph citation)](https://www.allmusic.com/artist/divine-mn0000797670) — Search excerpt gives a 1979 date for “Born to Be Cheap,” conflicting with the official discography and label history. The full profile was not retrieved; the older date is not used for the graph period.

## Assessment

Changed the music-activity start from 1979 to 1981. The previous citation was a biography page; AllMusic’s visible excerpt also gives 1979 for the first single, but Divine’s official discography and an independent label history both date the single to 1981. The latter year is the earliest corroborated recording milestone. Official Charts and the producers’ history independently substantiate the 1984 UK hit. Retained 1988 as the end of active career based on contemporary obituaries; later listed releases are posthumous. No phases were assigned because the evidenced recording career is short and separate from Divine’s longer acting and stage career.

## Remaining uncertainty

AllMusic gives 1979 for “Born to Be Cheap,” while the official discography and Red Bull Music Academy label history support 1981. The graph follows the corroborated 1981 discographic date; a 1979 club-performance milestone, if included, would be a different kind of career start.
